A second vessel hit in the same strike is still on fire and expected to go down, as Bulgaria's president convenes her national security council.
Bulgaria has called off the search for the 10 crew of a cargo ship sunk in a drone attack in the Black Sea, after finding no trace of the sailors.

ADVERTISEMENT
The operation ended at 5pm local time (4pm CET) on Tuesday and will not resume, Rumen Nikolov, head of rescue operations at Bulgaria's Maritime Administration, told reporters in Varna on Wednesday.
Searchers found three life rafts and an overturned lifeboat from the 83-metre long Alfa Watan but no one from its crew, he said, adding that no trace of the sailors was likely to be found.
The second ship hit in the attack, the Able, will probably sink as well, Nikolov said, according to shipping news site Maritime.bg. Its engine room flooded after a sea drone struck the stern, it is sitting heavily down at the stern and it was still burning on Wednesday morning.
Pollution in the area is minimal, he said.
Bulgaria President Iliana Iotova has convened the country's national security council to discuss maritime security.
All 18 of the Able's crew, 11 Turkish and seven Indian, were rescued by the Bulgarian ferry Dioscuria and are now in Varna. Two were hospitalised, one with burns and one with a head injury.
Sea and aerial drones hit the ships at about 3am local time (2am CET) on Tuesday, around 70 nautical miles east of the town of Byala in Bulgaria's exclusive economic zone, Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev said.
The crew of the Dioscuria, which was in the area, saw an explosion on the first ship and rushed to the scene, Radev confirmed. They found an overturned lifeboat and life jackets but no one from the crew.
Radev said two helicopters were sent to Varna to pick up rescue teams and a Su-25 aircraft was deployed to carry out aerial reconnaissance over the area.
Both vessels had sent distress signals during the night and one disappeared from Border Police radar at about 9am local time (8am CET) on Tuesday, public broadcaster BNR reported.
Both vessels are Turkish-owned, according to the Equasis merchant shipping database and it is believed to be the first attack on shipping in Bulgaria's exclusive economic zone.
Ship tracking website vesselfinder.com shows
the Alfa Watan was sailing under the flag of Togo
and was headed for the Romanian port of Sulina.
The second ship was carrying wheat and sailing south, Radev said. The cargo of the sunken Alfa Watan is not yet known.
Radev, who called the attack "absolutely unacceptable", did not say who launched the drones, while European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen called the developments "concerning".
Three attacks on civilian shipping in the Black Sea within two days
Tuesday's attack occurred the day after a
Turkish-owned cargo ship
carrying grain from Ukraine to Italy was attacked off Romania's coast in the Black Sea by at least one drone and sank after catching fire, killing two crew members.
Azerbaijan's Foreign Ministry said the ship's captain, Azerbaijani national Rustam Ismayil oglu Hasanov, died and his body was being repatriated. A second Azerbaijani crew member, Mahammad Hamza oglu Suleymanzade, is still missing and the search and rescue operation is ongoing. Azerbaijan's Foreign Ministry said their families were informed and they were receiving logistical and financial support.
11 other crew members were rescued by the Romanian authorities. Three Azerbaijani sailors and one Indian crew member among them are receiving emergency medical care in Romania.
Azerbaijan's Foreign Ministry explicitly reiterated its urgent warning to Azerbaijani citizens to refrain from travelling to active conflict zones, working in high-risk areas, or taking employment on transport vessels operating along contested maritime corridors.
Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelenskyy blamed Russia for yesterday's sinking of the cargo ship and called it "a horrific strike by two Russian drones on a civilian vessel" in neutral waters.
India condemned Tuesday's attack as part of the crew members were Indian nationals. The Indian Foreign Ministry released a statement saying that "India views these attacks on commercial shipping and seafarers with grave concern," adding that "the targeting of traffic in international waterways is severely undermining global commerce and welfare of people across the world."
