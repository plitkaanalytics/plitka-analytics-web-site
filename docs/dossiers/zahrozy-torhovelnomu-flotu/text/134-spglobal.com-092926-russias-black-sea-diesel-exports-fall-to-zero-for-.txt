Refined Products, Gasoline, Diesel-Gasoil, Jet Fuel, Naphtha

Russia's Black Sea diesel exports fall to zero for first time on record
By
Kelly Norways
Editor:
Karina Roman
Getting your
Trinity Audio
player ready...
HIGHLIGHTS
Black Sea diesel/gasoil shipments fall from 66,000 mt
Total exports down by almost 50% week over week
Export ban not due to expire until end of October
Russia's Black Sea diesel and gasoil shipments fell to zero for the first time on record in the week to Sept. 24, according to S&P Global
Commodities at Sea
data, as a nationwide export ban has continued to hammer trade flows from the major global producer.
Exemptions for inter-governmental agreements have previously stopped Russia's Black Sea shipments from falling to zero. In the week to Sept. 17, some 66,000 mt of Russian diesel/gasoil left the port of Novorossiysk, up from a previous five-week average of 51,000 mt. At the same time last year, Black Sea ports would dispatch around 200,000 mt per week.
The halt in exports coincided with a broader national slump, which saw total Russian diesel/gasoil shipments almost halve during the week to total 81,000 mt. The product was exclusively loaded at the Baltic Sea port of Primorsk and showed as destined for Morocco and Venezuela. In contrast, exports were directed to at least five destinations the previous week, including Turkey, Morocco and Singapore.
In the month-to-date, Russia has shipped 448,000 mt of diesel/gasoil to various destinations, CAS data shows, down from 535,000 mt in August and 713,000 mt in July.
Other oil products shipments included 197,000 mt of unspecified clean products, all from Far East and Baltic Sea loading terminals, as well as 227,000 mt of naphtha, CAS data showed. No jet fuel or gasoline departed the country by sea for the second consecutive week.
Slumping export volumes coincide with newly-renewed commitments from the Kremlin to keep restricting shipments, after the government announced Sept. 14 that it would
extend its export ban
through the end of October. Recent refinery attacks have kept pressure on the domestic sector, with the country's Ilsky, Slavyansk, Perm and Novoshakhtinsk facilities all recent targets of Ukrainian drones.
The trend has continued to capture the attention of US President Donald Trump, who on Sept. 27
reiterated calls
for Ukraine to slow the pace of its attacks. In comments to Fox News, the president blamed the disruption for soaring diesel prices, adding that he had spoken with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, asking him to "take it easy on the refineries."
"The big problem is the refineries in Russia are being blown up. This isn't really a Middle Eastern problem; this is more of a Russia problem," Trump said.
The Russian domestic supply situation has compounded pressure on the global market by cutting flows to key export destinations, but also by transforming the country into a net fuel importer. For the second week running, Russia imported nearly 40,000 mt of unspecified clean products from the Moroccan port of Tanger Med, according to CAS data.
After months of growing scrutiny over the state of the Russian refining sector, the country's president, Vladimir Putin, signed a decree on Sept. 28 that restricts access to and distribution of a wide range of energy data, including details on refinery throughput, export and logistics traffic. In addition, information about planned gasoline and diesel sales on the exchange floor will be restricted, according to the notice on the Kremlin's website.
