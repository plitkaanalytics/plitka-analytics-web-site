Ukraine and Moldova agree on alternative routes after Russian strike on Dniester bridge
27 December 2025, 04:09 PM
Europe
Read NV on Google
The bridge in Maiaky that was attacked by Russian forces (Photo: Yuliia Svyrydenko / Telegram)
After a Russian strike damaged a bridge over the Dniester River, Ukraine and Moldova agreed on alternative transport routes to maintain connectivity for southern Odesa Oblast, Deputy Prime Minister for European and Euro-Atlantic Integration Taras Kachka said in an interview with
European Pravda.
Kachka said Ukrainian authorities, including the Ministry for Communities and Territorial Development, customs officials, and border guards, immediately contacted their Moldovan counterparts after the strike on the bridge near Maiaky.
“The response from the Moldovan government was immediate and effective,” Kachka said.
Ad
According to the deputy prime minister, the two sides identified several options to address the disruption, including expanding the capacity of existing border crossings and, if necessary, establishing new transit routes.
Kachka added that Ukraine and Moldova also resolved issues related to the passage of drivers carrying only Ukrainian documents.
“Fortunately, we did not have to activate this scenario because normal traffic on Ukrainian roads was restored. But the mechanism is ready. If needed, a convoy will be organized to escort Ukrainian transport. All procedures with Moldova have been agreed, and I am extremely grateful to our Moldovan colleagues,” he said.
Earlier, Deputy Prime Minister for Restoration and Minister for Communities and Territorial Development
Oleksii Kuleba
said Moldova had agreed to increase the number of border crossings open to buses and cargo trucks due to logistical difficulties in southwestern Odesa Oblast, said on Dec. 20.
Russia launched an overnight attack on Ukraine using one Iskander-M ballistic missile and 99 drones, with confirmed hits by the missile and 26 drones at 16 locations, reported overnight into Dec. 26.
Russian forces have been striking Odesa Oblast almost continuously for more than two weeks, targeting energy facilities and port infrastructure. Another overnight attack on ports and industrial sites in the region killed one person and injured two others, reported overnight into Dec. 25.
Freight traffic along the M-15 Odesa-Reni highway was suspended after Russian strikes damaged the Dniester bridge near Maiaky. Crossings along parts of the Ukraine-Moldova border in Odesa Oblast were also temporarily halted, reported on Dec. 19.
Ukrainian electronic warfare and communications expert
Serhii Beskrestnov, known by the call sign Flash, said Russian forces are attempting to cut off western Odesa Oblast from key transport routes and gain control over access to the Danube.
