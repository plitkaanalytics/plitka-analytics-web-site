Aframax Rio blaze underscores widening Black Sea threat
Sam Chambers
October 7, 2026
1
734
2 minutes read
X.com
X.com
The Aframax Rio was ablaze off Sochi on Tuesday after being struck by unmanned surface vessels, adding another tanker casualty to a rapidly worsening Black Sea security picture.
Russia’s transport ministry said the Liberia-flagged aframax was attacked off the Russian resort city, triggering a major fire and an oil spill. All 23 crew, all Indian nationals, were rescued and no injuries were reported. Images from the coast showed a large column of black smoke rising from the tanker and burning oil on the water.
The Aframax Rio is a tanker linked to Russian oil exports and listed under Ukrainian sanctions.
Local authorities temporarily closed beaches around Sirius and advised residents to limit time outdoors while air and water quality were monitored.
The attack comes as maritime security specialist Ambrey warns that the threat to merchant shipping is broadening sharply across the western Black Sea.
Three merchant vessels were struck in Romanian and Bulgarian exclusive economic zones within 24 hours on October 5-6, two of them sinking. All three had been trading to or from Danube River ports. Ambrey assessed it as highly likely that Russian forces carried out those attacks and said the incidents represent a “material escalation” because commercial ships are now exposed to kinetic activity inside the EEZs of two NATO members.
“The maritime domain of the conflict has widened, but the targeting profile has not changed,” Ambrey noted.
Splash reported on Monday that the Royad Mammadov sank off Romania after a suspected Russian drone strike, killing two seafarers, while Alfa Watan subsequently sank and Able caught fire off Bulgaria in separate incidents.
The pattern follows months of increasingly frequent attacks against commercial ships on both sides of the conflict.
In the hinterland, the Danube has effectively become part of the front line of Ukraine’s maritime export system, with attacks on merchant shipping and port infrastructure rising sharply as cargo has shifted away from Odesa, according to Ambrey.
Ambrey said the Danube’s share of cargo handled at Ukrainian ports jumped from around 10% in the first half of the year to 74% in August as repeated strikes degraded Odesa, Chornomorsk and Pivdennyi. Over the same period, attacks around the Danube ports of Izmail, Reni and Kiliia accelerated markedly, with July to September recording 3.7 times as many incidents per month as the first half of the year.
Since July, Ambrey has recorded at least nine merchant vessels struck on the Danube approaches, five of them in Romanian waters. That includes the October 5 attack on a grain carrier sailing from Izmail, which killed two crew members.
Odesa-cluster volumes fell from 7.9m tonnes in April to just 430,000 tonnes in August, while Danube cargo nearly doubled in a month to 1.24m tonnes. Port calls rose from 363 to 737, and grain loadings increased 3.5-fold.
“The Danube is no longer simply an alternative route. It is now part of the contested front line of Ukraine’s maritime trade,” the security firm noted in a new report. “Where the volumes go, the strikes follow, from the berth to the open sea.”
Congestion around Sulina is making matters worse. Ambrey said as many as 125 vessels were queued there on October 5 after weather disruption, while waits have stretched to 14–17 days. That concentrates ships in predictable positions within UAV range.
Insurance conditions are also deteriorating. Ambrey said many underwriters no longer offer cover for Ukrainian port calls, even as war-risk premiums have moved into double digits, while the Joint War Committee expanded the Black Sea Listed Area in September.
