# 2026 Iran war fuel crisis


| This article needs to be  updated.(June 2026) | 

The **2026 Iran war fuel crisis** is a worldwide fuel crisis caused by the [war between Iran and the U.S.-Israel coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war). The [closure of the Strait of Hormuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Strait_of_Hormuz_crisis), through which over 20% of the world's oil trade passes, and attacks on energy infrastructure in Iran and several [Gulf Cooperation Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Cooperation_Council) countries led to a large disruption in global oil supplies. [2] The disruption led to global oil prices rising

and caused fuel shortages in countries which imported most of their fuel from the

[[3]](#cite_note-cnbc-80-3)[Persian Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Gulf)region. Much of the world has been affected by

[panic buying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panic_buying)and severe disruption to the distribution of

[petroleum products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_products),

[liquefied natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquefied_natural_gas)(LNG), and

[urea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urea)used for

[fertiliser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertiliser).

The economies of most countries are expected to be adversely affected by the crisis, leading to [inflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflation) and heightened risks of [stagflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stagflation) and [recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recession).[[4]](#cite_note-economist-1-4) [5] As of April 2026 there were ongoing concerns about 

[energy security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_security)as well as

[food security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_security), related to fertiliser shortages and costs.

[Brent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brent_Crude) prices initially surged to a peak of approximately $118 in late March 2026 before declining to around $70 by July 1, then rebounded to over $100 by late July and fluctuated between $87 and $97 through August. [6] As of early September 2026, price surged up to $109 as renewed attacks on shipping and energy infrastructure reversed earlier gains.


[[7]](#cite_note-7)By June 2026, the yearly inflation rate is 3.53%, with the [consumer price index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_price_index) decreasing by 0.42% and weekly earnings increasing by 0.77% since May 2026. [8] In July, the 

[IMF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Monetary_Fund)revised its world

[GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_domestic_product)growth rate to 3% for 2026 and 3.4% in 2027.

According to the same report, the effects of the oil supply shock on the world economy are unevenly distributed, weighing more on energy importers and vulnerable economies.

[[9]](#cite_note-weo-update-9)

[[9]](#cite_note-weo-update-9)## Overview

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=1)]

The 2026 Iran war caused immediate volatility in energy markets, with Brent crude oil prices surging 10–13% to around $80–82 per barrel by 2 March 2026, [3] and dropping back to near $70 by July, 

[2026](https://www.marketwatch.com/investing/future/brn00?countrycode=uk). The conflict has caused the restriction of nearly all traffic through the Strait of Hormuz, leading to what the

[International Energy Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Energy_Agency)has characterised as the "largest supply disruption in the history of the global oil market".


[[11]](#cite_note-nyt-echoes-11)The head of the IEA described the situation caused by the war as the "greatest global energy security challenge in history".

[[2]](#cite_note-omr-2)

[[12]](#cite_note-12)Iran's closure of the [Strait of Hormuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Hormuz) disrupted 20% of global oil supplies and significant [liquefied natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquefied_natural_gas) (LNG) volumes.[[13]](#cite_note-13) [14] Analysts have forecast that prices could reach $100 per barrel if disruptions persisted, potentially adding 0.8% to global inflation.


[[15]](#cite_note-15)Even after Iran and the United States [announced a ceasefire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war_ceasefire) on 8 April, ship traffic through the Strait of Hormuz remained far below pre-war levels.[[16]](#cite_note-guardian-ceasefire-16)[[17]](#cite_note-cbs-ceasefire-17)

Exports from the region have been typically going to Asian countries, with China, India, Japan, and South Korea accounting for 75% of oil and 59% of LNG exports.[[18]](#cite_note-18) [19] However, Singapore and Taiwan depend more on Qatari LNG, while Pakistan and Bangladesh are more price sensitive.


[[20]](#cite_note-20)Qatari state-owned oil and gas company [QatarEnergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QatarEnergy) announced on 3 March that it was declaring [Force Majeure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_Majeure) on its contracts with buyers, and internal sources, according to [Reuters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuters), said that it would soon be shutting down gas liquefication, as LNG tankers could not leave the Gulf, and that restarting it would take weeks. [21] These announcements caused increases in world gas prices, which analysts said was a part of the 

[Iranian Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Government)'s plan to apply pressure on the world to stop the war.

On 6 March, Qatar warned that if the war continues, other Gulf energy producers may be forced to halt exports and declare Force Majeure.

[[22]](#cite_note-22)Also on 6 March, it was reported that according to satellite imagery analysis by both

[[23]](#cite_note-23)[Bloomberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomberg_News)and the Energy Economics and Society Research Institute in

[Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo), that

[Ras Laffan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ras_Laffan_gas_processing_plant), the main gas facility in Qatar, appears not to have been damaged before the "unprecedented shutdown" which sent fuel prices higher.

The United States, buffered by domestic production, faced less direct impact but saw petrol prices rise 5–10 cents per gallon daily.

[[24]](#cite_note-24)

[[25]](#cite_note-NPR-25)On 18 March, Iran hit Qatar's inactive [Ras Laffan Industrial City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ras_Laffan_Industrial_City) LNG complex, causing a 17% reduction in Qatar's LNG production capacity. [26] The damages from this attack would take 3–5 years to fix.

Consequently, LNG spot prices in Asia increased by over 140 %.

[[27]](#cite_note-27)

[[28]](#cite_note-28)The impacts of the conflict are similar to the [1970s energy crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970s_energy_crisis), including acute supply shortages, currency volatility, inflation, and heightened risks of [stagflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stagflation) and [recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recession).[[11]](#cite_note-nyt-echoes-11)[[29]](#cite_note-El_Safty-2026-29)[[30]](#cite_note-30)[[31]](#cite_note-Reuters-31)

According to the International Energy Agency's World Energy Investment 2026 report, the crisis was prompting a reshaping of global energy investment and accelerating diversification away from Middle Eastern supply routes. The agency projected total energy investment of $3.4 trillion in 2026, with oil investment falling for a third consecutive year to below $500 billion, while natural gas investment rose to $330 billion—its highest in a decade—driven by new LNG projects in the United States and Qatar. Coal investment was projected to reach $180 billion, the highest since 2012. IEA Executive Director Fatih Birol described the situation as "the largest energy security crisis the world has ever faced", drawing a parallel with the oil shocks of the 1970s.[[32]](#cite_note-32)

Countries such as Australia and India have more sufficient reserves but face challenges with panic buying.[[33]](#cite_note-ndtv-fuel-panic-33)[[34]](#cite_note-34)[[4]](#cite_note-economist-1-4)[[35]](#cite_note-35) [36] Economies most reliant on the strait for energy imports are in Asia, with Europe also viewing the strait as vital for its energy security. The initial disruption of petroleum is expected to affect Asia the most, but Europe is likely to be hit hard in the medium-to-long term, and the UK is expected to be the worst-hit major economy.


[[37]](#cite_note-37)

[[38]](#cite_note-38)The British think tank The Food Policy Institute has warned of long-term increases in food prices due to disruption in fuel and fertiliser markets. [39] The Strait of Hormuz is central to the global fertiliser trade. Over 30 per cent of global 

[urea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urea), which is widely used and produced from natural gas – is exported from Gulf countries through the Strait. Much of the cost of producing some foods, including corn and wheat, is in the cost of fertiliser, and this cost, along with rising energy costs, makes basic food production very expensive. Much of the Gulf states' food is imported – for example, over 90 per cent in Qatar. These factors have led to fears of

[food insecurity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_security)not only in Gulf states, but around the world.


[[40]](#cite_note-40)

[[41]](#cite_note-mpherson-41)As of 28 March, analysts fear that another hard oil crisis is unfolding that is expected to be very decisive to the global economy.[[42]](#cite_note-42)

Oil prices increased on September 13 due to market stability after recent declines. Investors are awaiting potential U.S.-Iran talks at the UN General Assembly. Analysts believe the price rise may result from traders adjusting their positions rather than a change in market trends.[[43]](#cite_note-43)

### Airlines

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=2)]

Airlines are being affected by the shortage of [jet fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_fuel) in parts of Asia and Oceania. Many airlines in southeast Asia and Oceania are either adding surcharges to fares or cancelling flights or routes. Australia, which sources most of its jet fuel from China, followed by Singapore and South Korea, has 30 days' worth of jet fuel held in reserve.[[44]](#cite_note-whiting-44)

South Korea makes the refined product and exports it around the region, but it relies mostly on crude arriving via the Gulf, so it is considering limiting its exports. Korean Air is the country's third airline to enter "emergency mode", at this point focusing only on internal cost-reduction measures.[[44]](#cite_note-whiting-44)

## Africa

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=3)]

### Algeria

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=4)]

Algeria is an [oil and natural gas producer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_in_Algeria), and one of the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union)'s largest suppliers of LNG. It has entered talks with Spain and Italy, as well as fielding enquiries from other countries, including Vietnam. It could benefit economically.[[45]](#cite_note-wekesa-45)

### Egypt

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=5)]

Egypt relies on imported oil, so the government has introduced measures to keep fuel usage down, including the mandatory closure of retail businesses at 9pm for a month, dimming street lights and roadside advertisements. It has slowed down some large government projects, and increased the price of both petrol and public transport fares. Non-essential workers have been ordered to work from home one day per week.[[45]](#cite_note-wekesa-45)

### Ethiopia

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=6)]

Ethiopia is experiencing a severe fuel crisis, with over 180,000 tonnes of fuel undelivered in recent weeks. This has halved the country's daily diesel supply from 9.2 million to 4.5 million litres, according to Minister Kassahun Gofe. [46] In 

[Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia), security institutions have been prioritised, along with, major government projects, key industries, and essential goods production. There are fuel restrictions, and petrol stations are prioritising public transport. Fuel supplies to the

[Tigray Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigray_Region)have been completely suspended.

Addis Ababa residents have reported that, “Over the past month, prices of many commodities have increased significantly ...bottled water, cooking oil, eggs and other essentials have risen by nearly 40 per cent.”

[[47]](#cite_note-bbcafrica-47)

[[48]](#cite_note-48)### Ghana

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=7)]

Ghana, which has limited refining capacity, relies on imported refined products, but is supplied by a range of producers, including Russia.[[45]](#cite_note-wekesa-45)

### Kenya

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=8)]

Kenya is badly affected by the war, as it sources all its oil from the Middle East. There has been panic buying of petrol, but the [Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_and_Petroleum_Regulatory_Authority) (EPRA) has maintained stability in prices for 30 days. The war has also affected some exports, with ships taking longer to reach their destination, so the Kenya Ports Authority has prioritised the export of perishable products such as tea, flowers, and avocados. [45] The fuel crisis has greatly impacted Kenya's important 

[floriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floriculture)industry.


[[47]](#cite_note-bbcafrica-47)The EPRA announced record high fuel price increases on 15 April. [49] This resulted in public transport operators hiking their fares by roughly 25% dependent on various factors.


[[50]](#cite_note-50)These actions are expected to raise inflation and increase prices for basic goods and important services.

[[51]](#cite_note-51)

[[52]](#cite_note-52)### Mauritius

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=9)]

Mauritius is dependent on oil imports for generating power, and, after a shipment of oil had not arrived as scheduled on 30 March, only 21 days of stock remained in the country. The government arranged for more expensive alternative fuel to be shipped from Singapore, and imposed restrictions to reduce wastage of fuel.[[47]](#cite_note-bbcafrica-47)

### Namibia

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=10)]

The Namibian Government reduced fuel levies by 50% for at least three months, using its National Energy Fund to stabilise prices until the end of June. Gas and oil exploration continues in the country, which expects to start producing oil by 2030.[[45]](#cite_note-wekesa-45)

### Nigeria

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=11)]

Nigeria is a [major oil producer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_industry_in_Nigeria), and its large [Dangote refinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dangote_refinery) has increased production to help meet world shortages. [45] Despite the government and oil companies profiting from this, ordinary citizens are not expected to benefit immediately, due to transport cost increases.


[[47]](#cite_note-bbcafrica-47)### South Africa

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=12)]

Saudi Arabia supplies most of South Africa's fuel. [53] The 

[South African Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Government)has said that there was no shortage of fuel in the country, but prices have increased and some petrol stations have introduced their own rationing of diesel. With more traffic potentially taking the route around the

[Cape of Good Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_of_Good_Hope), and ships needing to fuel along the African coast, some ports such as

[Walvis Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walvis_Bay),

[Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town), and

[Durban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durban)may come under increased pressure, but also benefit from the diversion.


[[47]](#cite_note-bbcafrica-47)### Uganda

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=13)]

At the end of March, Uganda only had a few weeks' worth of stock.[[53]](#cite_note-1news-53)

### South Sudan

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=14)]

South Sudan has large oil reserves, but most of it is exported and it relies on imports for refined products. Because the country generates 96% of its electricity from oil, the government has started rationing electricity in the capital, [Juba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juba), which affects businesses, and rolling power cuts are expected to continue.[[45]](#cite_note-wekesa-45)[[47]](#cite_note-bbcafrica-47)

### Zimbabwe

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=15)]

Zimbabwe has announced plans to scrap some taxes on fuel imports, which rose 40% in under a month. It has increased the amount of ethanol in its petrol from 5% to 20%.[[45]](#cite_note-wekesa-45)[[47]](#cite_note-bbcafrica-47)

## Americas

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=16)]

### North America

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=17)]

Fuel prices across North America have gone up significantly, beginning in early March, due to the market conditions shifting rapidly as the crisis continues. Liquefied natural gas prices have largely remained the same since the beginning of the energy crisis due to the lack of connectivity in the North American liquefied natural gas market, with minimal pipelines and export points. Gas prices have risen $1.16 a gallon in the United States since the start of the war, with prices expected to hit $5.00 a gallon if the Strait of Hormuz is not opened by mid-April. [58] In California, a state heavily reliant on energy imports from Asia,

gasoline prices surpassed $6 a gallon in seven counties as of March 30.

[[59]](#cite_note-59)In Canada, gas prices have risen approximately 30 percent from March to April.

[[60]](#cite_note-60)

[[61]](#cite_note-61)[Jet fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_fuel)in North America has spiked 95 percent since the war began, causing multiple airlines to raise prices for

[checked baggage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Checked_baggage). Shipping services, such as the

[United States Postal Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Postal_Service),

[Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_(company)), and

[FedEx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FedEx)implemented fuel surcharges.


[[62]](#cite_note-62)On 24 April, U.S. exports of crude and petroleum products rose to nearly 12.9 million barrels a day.[[63]](#cite_note-63)

On 2 May 2026, [Spirit Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_Airlines) ceased all operations citing rising fuel costs despite the Trump administration's attempts to save the company.[[64]](#cite_note-64)

In September 2026, Costco began rationing motor oil to two boxes per customer per week. [65] The 

[Strategic Petroleum Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Petroleum_Reserve_(United_States))fell to its lowest levels since November 1982 as 172 million barrels were planned to be drawn down.


[[66]](#cite_note-66)According to the AAA, average diesel prices in the United States reached $6.53 on 22 September, up 77% from a year ago.[[67]](#cite_note-67)

## Asia

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=18)]

Asian countries are the primary destination for crude oil from the Gulf, with most of it travelling via the Strait of Hormuz. In 2024, around 84 per cent of the crude oil and 83 per cent of LNG passing through the Strait went to Asia; nearly 70 per cent of the oil went to China, India, Japan, and South Korea. [68] Governments and businesses across the region have been imposing measures to reduce the impact of the fuel crisis, and among the worst hit countries in the region include Pakistan, Bangladesh, and Vietnam.


[[68]](#cite_note-hale-68)

[[69]](#cite_note-69)### Bangladesh

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=19)]

Bangladesh is among the worst hit economies and is projected to have a severe impact on GDP growth inducing recession-like conditions.[[70]](#cite_note-70) [71] There have been fears of unrest due to the fuel crisis.


[[72]](#cite_note-72)

[[73]](#cite_note-73)

[[74]](#cite_note-74)Universities were closed in advance for the upcoming [Eid al-Fitr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eid_al-Fitr) holidays in order to conserve electricity and fuel. [75] All shopping centres and commercial establishments have also been directed to shut down by 8.00 pm.

This was later revised to 6 pm, before being increased to 7 pm and extended the restrictions to cultural events.

[[76]](#cite_note-76)

[[77]](#cite_note-77)

[[78]](#cite_note-78)Bangladesh relies on coal for much of its power, and has ramped up power generation from coal as well as importing more power created from coal.[[79]](#cite_note-ratcliffe-79)

### China

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=20)]

As of March 2025, the government is estimated to have 401 million barrels stored in above-ground facilities and 668 million barrels in above-ground commercial storage. [80] By December 2025, United States 

[Energy Information Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_Information_Administration)estimated Chinese held around 1.4 billion barrels of crude oil in reserve from both government-held and state-owned national oil companies (NOC) inventories, which are both part of the strategic reserves.

Outside of the official strategic reserves, Chinese corporate and commercial refineries also have several hundred million barrels of crude oil.

[[81]](#cite_note-81)China hiked prices serveal times during the war, but due to queue forming in the gas stations in multiple Chinese cities, the

[[82]](#cite_note-nyt_260615-82)[Chinese government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_government)reduced its planned fuel price hikes.


[[83]](#cite_note-bbc-china-price-83)China's large strategic reserve helped the country insulate itself significantly from the [2026 Iran war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war) and ensuing global fuel crisis.[[84]](#cite_note-84)[[85]](#cite_note-85)[[86]](#cite_note-86)

By late June 2026, China's strategic petroleum reserve (SPR) remained nearly unchanged after the Iran war. [87] To ensure domestic supplies without using strategic reserve, China halted its supplies of refined oil products to other countries.


[[87]](#cite_note-nyt_260621-87)China is a major [oil refiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_refiner) and exporter of petroleum products. On 5 March, major Chinese refiners, including [China Petroleum & Chemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Petroleum_&_Chemical) (Sinopec) and Rongsheng Petrochemical Co Ltd, were ordered by the government to stop accepting new fuel export contracts. [Jet fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_fuel) refuelling for international flights and supplies to [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong) and [Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macau) are exempt from this guidance.[[88]](#cite_note-reuters-china-refine-88)[[89]](#cite_note-bloomberg-china-refine-89)

During the crisis, China primarily tapped into its corporate stockpiles, outside its official SPR of 1.23 billion barrels, to supply domestic needs. [87] There could also be other previously unreported reserves in China.

These corporate reserves can be used at one million barrels per day for a year. China's growing

[[90]](#cite_note-vox-90)[renewable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy)sector and

[electric vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_vehicle)industry have also reduced the demand for petroleum products.


[[82]](#cite_note-nyt_260615-82)Although China restricted overall exports of refined oil products during the war, it still supplied limited volumes to selected partner countries, either due to friendly relations or to secure diplomatic leverage.  The conflict has also provided China with an opportunity to expand its [renewable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy) technologies in Asia.[[91]](#cite_note-91)

### India

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=21)]

India imports around half of its crude oil from Middle Eastern countries. [92] On 27 March, the 

[Indian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_government)reduced

[excise duties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excise_Duty_(India))on petrol and diesel by

[₹](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_rupee)10 (10¢ US) per litre to keep fuel prices from rising.


[[93]](#cite_note-cnbc-india-tax-93)Unfounded panic about shortages led to people forming long queues for petrol.

[[94]](#cite_note-firstpost-india-tax-94)

[[33]](#cite_note-ndtv-fuel-panic-33)India is the 4th largest country in the world by oil refining capacity and throughput. [95] Indian refiners started buying petroleum from Russia as the war disrupted supplies from the Middle East.

To ensure domestic availability of fuel, the government raised export duties to ₹21.5 (22¢ US) per litre on diesel and to ₹29.5 (31¢ US) per litre on aviation fuel.

[[96]](#cite_note-reuters-india-russia-96)

[[93]](#cite_note-cnbc-india-tax-93)

[[94]](#cite_note-firstpost-india-tax-94)[Liquefied petroleum gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquefied_petroleum_gas) (LPG) is the main cooking fuel for households and restaurants in India. 60% of India's LPG demand is fulfilled by imports, most of which pass through the Hormuz Strait. [97] It was the first fuel to be affected by the crisis, leading to long queues and delayed deliveries.


[[98]](#cite_note-bbc-lpg-india-98)Many people are using fuels like

[[99]](#cite_note-indiatoday-lpg-99)[kerosene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerosene), coal and wood as stopgap measures.

In the month of March 2026, India installed piped gas connections to 580,000 new households.

[[100]](#cite_note-bbc-india-fuel-100)Piped gas in India is mainly sourced from

[[101]](#cite_note-retures-pipe-india-101)[domestic gas fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_and_gas_industry_in_India), therefore its supply was unaffected by the war.


[[102]](#cite_note-intoday-pipedgas-102)Nearly 75% of [electricity in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_in_India) is generated from coal. India plans to increase coal power to meet high energy demands in the summer amid the oil and gas crisis.[[103]](#cite_note-reuters-coal-india-103)[[79]](#cite_note-ratcliffe-79)

In [Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarat) state, the gas shortage has led to its ceramics industry shutting down. [97] In 

[Mumbai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai), many restaurants shut down fully or partially in early March, due to the lack of cooking gas.


[[97]](#cite_note-ewe-97)### Indonesia

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=22)]

Indonesia, although [an oil producer itself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_in_Indonesia#Oil), imports around a third of its supply. As the largest economy in [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia), it keeps a fuel reserve of around 22 days.[[68]](#cite_note-hale-68)

The shortage in fuel imports have caused prices for unsubsidized fuel to increase by up to 32%, as well as a surge in spending for fuel, electricity, and fertilizers subsidies to double that of last year.[[104]](#cite_note-104)

### Japan

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=23)]

As of February 2026, 94.2% of Japan's crude oil imports came from the Middle East. [105] However, the impacts of the oil supply disruption due to the strait closure were partly mitigated by its large stockpiles.


[[106]](#cite_note-106)On 16 March, the [Government of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Japan) started releasing 80 million barrels of oil, equivalent to 15 days of domestic demand, from its strategic reserves.[[107]](#cite_note-japan-oil-release-107)

### Myanmar

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=24)]

Myanmar, which has experienced [civil war since 2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar_civil_war_(2021–present)), has restricted private vehicle use to alternate days, and there are long queues at petrol stations. [97] The country is lacking in refineries, and it relies on oil products imported from Thailand, Vietnam, and 

[Singapore](#Singapore).


[[68]](#cite_note-hale-68)### Nepal

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=25)]

The executive director of the state-run [Nepal Oil Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal_Oil_Corporation) announced that, as of March 13, authorities would refill only half of consumers' empty cylinders to lengthen liquid petroleum stockpiles.[[108]](#cite_note-108)

### Philippines

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=26)]

| This article needs to be  updated.(September 2026) | 

Since late February 2026, the closure of the [Strait of Hormuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Hormuz) during the [2026 Iran war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war) disrupted roughly 20 percent of the world's oil supply, triggering a [global economic downturn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_impact_of_the_2026_Iran_war) and fuel shortages. [109] The Philippines, which imports most of its oil from the 

[Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East), was particularly vulnerable to supply disruptions because of its heavy dependence on

[imported petroleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_oil_imports).


[[109]](#cite_note-2026_Philippine_energy_crisis_BBC-109)On March 23, [Claire Castro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claire_Castro), press officer of the [Office of the President of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_President_of_the_Philippines), said the country was experiencing a "price disruption" caused by the conflict in the Middle East but was not yet facing an oil crisis. Her remarks were echoed by Energy Secretary [Sharon Garin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Garin). [110] The same day, President 

[Bongbong Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bongbong_Marcos)ordered the creation of a crisis committee to maintain economic stability and ensure the continued delivery of essential resources, including supplies for schools.


[[111]](#cite_note-111)On March 24, Marcos declared a state of national energy emergency, stating that the country had sufficient crude oil supplies until June 30. [112] The Philippines became the first country to declare an energy emergency in response to the war.


[[109]](#cite_note-2026_Philippine_energy_crisis_BBC-109)According to the [Philippine Institute for Development Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Institute_for_Development_Studies) (PIDS), the energy crisis could push between 1.3 million and 3.1 million Filipinos into poverty.[[113]](#cite_note-113)

The Philippines declared a state of emergency on 24 March due to a concurrent strike by transport workers, [114] and government offices in the Philippines are working only four days per week.


[[68]](#cite_note-hale-68)### Singapore

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=27)]

[Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore) is an important hub for the passage of oil; it receives crude oil, mostly from the Middle East, and its refineries (most of which are on the 32-square-kilometre (12 sq mi) [Jurong Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurong_Island)) produce the products which are then exported around the [Asia-Pacific region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia-Pacific_region), processing up to 1.5 million barrels of crude per day. Singapore and Australia made an agreement to keep their energy trade flowing, and Singapore economic officials have said that the country's refineries are maintaining stable operations and drawing on diverse supplies of crude oil.[[115]](#cite_note-dingwall-115)

### South Korea

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=28)]

The crisis has not had a dramatic impact on the lives of most South Koreans, and energy minister [Kim Sung-hwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Sung-hwan_(politician)?action=edit&redlink=1) has said that the country would not experience problems with supply for over a year. However, the [Korean Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_South_Korea) launched an energy-saving campaign, encouraging its citizens to have brief showers, and limit their use of washing machines, [97] and it has postponed the decommissioning of coal-fired power plants as well as reducing the limit on electricity made from coal.


[[79]](#cite_note-ratcliffe-79)### Sri Lanka

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=29)]

Sri Lanka has introduced a four-day working week. In addition, there are long queues for petrol, causing people to miss out on work.[[79]](#cite_note-ratcliffe-79)[[97]](#cite_note-ewe-97)

### Thailand

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=30)]

Thailand has been severely impacted by the crisis. Thailand imports 57% of its oil from the [Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East), which requires to pass the [Strait of Hormuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Hormuz). The [diesel fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_fuel) price has increased from 29.94 [THB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_baht) per litre in February, up to peak price of 50.54 THB per litre in 7 April.[[116]](#cite_note-116)

The government froze diesel prices at 29.94 THB per litre in the first 15 days of the crisis, [117] and has directed people to raise their air-conditioning temperatures and reduce their outer clothing, to reduce energy consumption, and employees of all government agencies are 

[working from home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_from_home).


[[118]](#cite_note-118)[Phipat Ratchakitprakarn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phipat_Ratchakitprakarn), [Deputy Prime Minister of Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Thailand) and co-owner of PTG Energy (branded as PT), one of the largest oil companies in Thailand, was assigned by [Anutin Charnvirakul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anutin_Charnvirakul), [Prime Minister of Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Thailand) to control and mitigate the impact of fuel crisis in Thailand. He has been criticised by Thai citizens for his failure to manage the impact, and the "Ban PT" movement has gained traction across the internet.[[119]](#cite_note-119)

In [Pathum Thani Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathum_Thani_Province), north of Bangkok, the local [Provincial Administrative Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Administrative_Organization) (PAO) has terminated its 4.45 [satang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satang) per litre (Equivalent to 0.445 THB) fuel dispenser tax for 3 months, in 317 stations across the province. Normally, this tax generates 4 million THB per month for the PAO. By this measure the PAO hopes to reduce fuel costs for the residents of Pathum Thani Province, especially for farmers.[[120]](#cite_note-120)

On 3 April, Ruthaphon Naowarat, Minister of Justice, stated in CCSA Press Conference that the [Ministry of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Justice_(Thailand)) had detected 57 million litres of fuel missing from 217 million litres transported by sea to six oil depots in [Surat Thani province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surat_Thani_province), only 160 million litres arrived at destination. This has created controversies regarding government responsibilities.[[121]](#cite_note-121)

On 4 April, [Bangchak Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangchak_Corporation), the third largest oil company in Thailand with around 2200 petrol stations nationwide, announced the *"Fry to Fly"* Program, which offers customers to exchange 2 litres of used [cooking oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooking_oil) for 1 litre of diesel fuel or [petrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrol) (up to 20 litres of cooking oil per person) at 15 stations across the [Bangkok Metropolitan Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangkok_Metropolitan_Region). This program reduces customers' fuel costs and allows Bangchak to use the used cooking oil to produce [sustainable aviation fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_aviation_fuel) (SAF). The program will be in effect from 6 to 30 April.[[122]](#cite_note-thaipbs-fly-to-fry-122)[[123]](#cite_note-thairath-fry-to-fly-123)

On 8 April, the Energy Policy Administration Committee announced on the [Royal Gazette of Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Gazette_(Thailand)) that diesel prices would be cut at the refinery level by 2 THB per litre, aiming to reduce fuel costs and cost of living for Thai citizens. This reduction includes Diesel B0, Diesel B7 and Diesel B20 blends, effective from 9 April. This resulted in diesel fuel prices dropping from 50.54 THB per litre on 7 April, to 48.40 THB per litre on 9 April. [124] On 11 April prices dropped to 44.40 THB per litre for normal diesel and to 37.40 THB per litre for Diesel B20.


[[125]](#cite_note-125)### Vietnam

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=31)]

Vietnam is among the most fragile of South East Asian countries due to low energy security, relying on countries such as China and Thailand for petroleum imports. [126] Vietnam is among the worst hit in South East Asia, with only Laos and Cambodia facing a worse situation.


[[127]](#cite_note-127)The economic impact of the fuel crisis is expected to slow Vietnam's economic growth.  [128] The fuel crisis has had a widespread impact on Vietnamese society as people change habits and find new ways of production.

Gig workers were especially hard hit by the doubling price of fuel.

[[129]](#cite_note-129)

[[130]](#cite_note-130)The [government of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Vietnam) has abolished some fuel levies until mid-April, but fuel prices are still way above what they were before the war. Increased shipping costs for local businesses have hurt them economically, with customers dropping off.[[97]](#cite_note-ewe-97)

## Europe

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=32)]

Primarily through the suspension of Qatari [liquefied natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquefied_natural_gas) (LNG) and the closure of the [Strait of Hormuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Hormuz), [131] several publications including the

[2022 energy crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_energy_crisis_(2021–2023))that followed the War in Ukraine.

The disruption contributed to sharp increases in global energy prices. Reports indicated that oil prices rose above 100 USD per barrel, while jet fuel prices increased significantly, in some cases more than doubling compared to the previous month. Industry groups and analysts warned that continued disruption could lead to operational challenges at European airports, including potential flight cancellations and capacity constraints. The International Air Transport Association (IATA) noted that even in the event of a reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, recovery in jet fuel supply could take months due to persistent constraints in refining capacity and logistics across the region.[[153]](#cite_note-153)[[154]](#cite_note-154)

In response to the rising energy prices, the [European Central Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Central_Bank) and the [Bank of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_England) left interest rates unchanged.[[155]](#cite_note-155)

This measure was enacted to curb drivers from neighbouring countries such as Austria taking advantage of lower fuel prices in Slovenia.[[158]](#cite_note-bbc-slovenia-158)

### United Kingdom

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=36)]

Shipping disruption contributed to volatility in UK energy markets, with analysts warning that wholesale gas price increases could raise household energy bills and expose the country's reliance on global fuel markets. [159] The situation also renewed political debate over the role of 

[domestic production in the North Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea_oil), including potential reforms to the UK's Energy Profits Levy (

However, analysts noted that additional drilling in the North Sea would be unlikely to significantly reduce UK energy bills in the short term, as most oil and gas produced there is sold on international markets at global prices.

[[161]](#cite_note-161)April 2026 reached the highest level of

[[162]](#cite_note-162)[solar panel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_panel)installations in the United Kingdom since 2012.


[[146]](#cite_note-jacobin-speeding-146)## Middle East

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=37)]

According to the International Energy Agency (IEA), the global natural gas market has been significantly affected by geopolitical disruptions in the Middle East, resulting in a short-term supply shock and reduced availability of liquefied natural gas (LNG) in global markets. The agency reported that these developments have tightened global gas balances and increased price volatility. While global LNG supply growth is expected to continue in the medium term, the market is projected to remain tight through 2026 and 2027, depending on geopolitical conditions and infrastructure recovery.[[163]](#cite_note-163)

## Oceania

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=38)]

### Australia

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=39)]

Fuel prices at the petrol and diesel stations started soaring in early March in Australia, partly driven by panic buying. More critical to overall inflationary forces though, is the supply of diesel to drive heavy machinery. Australia's diesel is largely dependent on refineries in Singapore, South Korea, Malaysia, Taiwan and India.[[164]](#cite_note-eia-aus-164) [165] Experts have warned that the increased price of diesel would lead to much higher food prices, threatening both food security and the "just-in-time" logistics infrastructure that serves food retailers.

Australia holds around 29 to 36 days' reserves of fuel,

[[41]](#cite_note-mpherson-41)[and a large proportion of this was released to support retailers in regional areas. If the Strait of Hormuz continues to be blocked, the

*]*[as of?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items)*Liquid Fuel Emergency Act 1984*could be triggered, which allows for formal transaction limits and rationing of fuel.

This act was passed in response to the

[[41]](#cite_note-mpherson-41)[1970s fuel crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970–1979_world_oil_market_chronology), and has not been triggered since then.

In the meantime, the government has lowered the standard for the

[[166]](#cite_note-166)[flash point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flash_point)for both diesel and petrol, which will make it easier for the country's two refineries to produce more fuel.


[[167]](#cite_note-167)In mid-March, the state governments of [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_State_Government) and [Tasmania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasmanian_Government) made public transport free to all users, for differing fixed time periods. On 30 March 2026, the [Australian Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Government) under Prime Minister [Anthony Albanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Albanese), after a meeting of [National Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Cabinet_(Australia)), announced a National Fuel Security Plan, to coordinate responses across all states and territories of Australia. The four-stage plan specifies roles and responsibilities of governments and industry partners. At this point, the country was at level two, dubbed "Keeping Australia moving", after going through a preparation and planning phase. The federal government announced the cutting of fuel excise by 50%, along with a three-month pause on road user charges for trucks.[[168]](#cite_note-168)

Singapore and Australia have signed an agreement to keep their energy trade flowing, with Singapore diversifying the origins of its supplies of crude oil.[[115]](#cite_note-dingwall-115)

### New Zealand

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=40)]

On 12 March, the [New Zealand Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Government) released six days' worth of [petroleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum) following a global directive by the [International Energy Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Energy_Agency) to release 400 million barrels of petrol in response to supply disruptions caused by the [2026 Iran war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war).[[169]](#cite_note-169)

On 24 March, the New Zealand Government announced that about 143,000 working families with children would receive a $50 tax credit to help with rising fuel costs from 7 April. Another 14,000 families were also expected to be eligible for a lower tax credit.[[170]](#cite_note-170)

On 27 March, the New Zealand Government released its four-level fuel alert level system in response to fuel shortages caused by the Iran conflict. That day the country was placed on the first phase, watchful, with the public advised to use fuel cautiously.[[171]](#cite_note-171)

### Other Pacific nations

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis&action=edit§ion=41)]

Several governments of Pacific nations, including [Samoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samoa), [Fiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiji), [Vanuatu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanuatu), and [Papua New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papua_New_Guinea), publicly reported by the end of March that they had enough fuel reserves to continue operating for a few months. However, some officials have privately expressed concerns about the future if oil deliveries to the region are halted owing to the war, and prices have gone up in all of these countries as well as [Solomon Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon_Islands), [Tonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonga), and [Marshall Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Islands). Officials in Samoa, [Cook Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Islands) and Fiji have warned citizens against panic buying and [hoarding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoarding_(economics)). [172] Australian Foreign Minister 

[Penny Wong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penny_Wong)and Minister for Pacific Island Affairs

[Pat Conroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Conroy_(politician))have signalled their willingness to ensure that regional neighbours do not run out of fuel. The

[New Zealand Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Government)stated that it was assessing how it could help the Pacific region, particularly the

[Polynesian nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesia).


[[173]](#cite_note-d056-173)Due to rising fuel costs, [Air New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_New_Zealand) has cancelled 1,100 flights in the Pacific region, affecting around 44,000 passengers.[[172]](#cite_note-pmn-pacific-crisis-172)
