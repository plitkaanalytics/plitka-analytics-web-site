Russian President Vladimir Putin is not considering an
energy
ceasefire with Ukraine, Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov said, claiming Moscow wants a broader peace agreement instead.
“
Russia
needs peace, not a ceasefire,” Peskov said, according to Russian state news agency
TASS
.

Peskov’s comments came as a rebuke to US President Donald Trump’s
recent announcement
of an energy truce between Kyiv and Moscow.
Peskov added that a full resolution of the war was impossible without Ukrainian officials’ participation.
“Someone there needs to make a responsible decision,” he said without specifying whom he meant.
Russian leader Vladimir Putin has reportedly
proposed a trilateral summit
with his US and Chinese counterparts in November to discuss a settlement in Ukraine without Kyiv’s participation.

Both the US and Russia also cast doubt on President Volodymyr Zelensky’s legitimacy despite most Ukrainians expressing trust in the leader in
recent polls
.
Peskov also commented on recent remarks by Finnish President Alexander Stubb, who said Ukrainian, US and European delegations identified new
ideas for ending the war
during Tuesday’s talks in Norway, ahead of Trump envoy Steve Witkoff’s visit to St. Petersburg.
Stubb said the proposals would be conveyed to the Kremlin for consideration.
“In my experience, peace requires patience, but unfortunately, war does not wait. People are dying every day. Our task is to do everything possible to put an end to this. And I believe that today was a step in the right direction,” Stubb said.

But Peskov dismissed the remarks once again, saying that simply repeating the general European position on Ukraine was “unlikely to be a constructive contribution to a settlement.”
Peace on whose terms?
While all parties – including the US and Europe – have called for peace in Ukraine, the terms have been polarizing.
Europe has sided with Kyiv, arguing that Russia is responsible for the invasion and that any peace should be
just and lasting
, rather than a temporary ceasefire – similar to Moscow’s wording, except that Europe says this is necessary to prevent another Russian invasion.
Advertisement
Russia has long cast its own Ukraine invasion as a war against NATO, initially demanding that Ukraine be demilitarized and barred from joining the alliance, and recently
reiterated demands
for Kyiv to voluntarily withdraw from eastern Ukraine’s Donbas region, to which Moscow has laid claims via unrecognized referendums.
Kyiv, meanwhile, called for a
freeze along the contact lines
, arguing that ceding the Donbas region could weaken its own security and allow Russia to
recuperate for another invasion
against the rest of Ukraine.
Ukraine’s recent
Operation Vivaldi
also reversed much of Russia’s gains in the region throughout 2026.
While the previous US administration backed Ukraine’s sovereignty, the Trump administration has
pursued economic incentives
to bring Moscow to the negotiating table, with Zelensky saying in March that Washington had
pressured Kyiv to give up the Donbas
in exchange for security guarantees.
Energy truce in words only
Trump had claimed on Oct. 11 that both Kyiv and Moscow had agreed to halt attacks on energy infrastructure. Neither Kyiv nor Moscow immediately confirmed the truce’s terms or how compliance would be monitored.
Advertisement
The announcement followed Zelensky’s statement that Kyiv was ready to suspend long-range drone strikes on Russian oil refineries if Moscow agreed to a reciprocal, verifiable halt to attacks on Ukraine’s electricity and heating networks.
The proposed truce came amid growing tensions between Washington and Kyiv after Trump announced a deal allowing Russian diesel sales and criticized Ukraine’s strikes on Russian refineries.
On Oct. 18, Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha accused Russia of
exploiting diplomatic efforts
while intensifying attacks on Ukraine’s energy infrastructure. He said Russian forces had carried out 76 targeted strikes on energy facilities in the week following Trump’s announcement.
During the same period, Russian forces launched nearly 2,700 strike drones, 1,700 guided aerial bombs and 66 missiles across Ukraine, Sybiha said.
Sybiha warned that easing sanctions or offering economic concessions without changes in Russia’s conduct would provide the Kremlin with additional revenue to finance its war. He urged Kyiv’s allies to increase pressure on Moscow rather than ease the economic costs of its aggression.
