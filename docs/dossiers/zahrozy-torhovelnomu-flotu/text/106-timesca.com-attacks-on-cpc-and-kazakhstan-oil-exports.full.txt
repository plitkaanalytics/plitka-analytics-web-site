Attacks on CPC and Kazakhstan’s Oil Exports
Timeline
Timeline
Attacks on the Caspian Pipeline Consortium have shifted from inland pumping stations to offshore loading equipment and civilian tankers. The first direct strike in this sequence came in February 2025. By July 2026, repeated incidents had halted Black Sea loadings and forced Kazakhstan to reduce production.
CPC carries more than 80% of Kazakhstan’s oil exports and includes major American investors. The growing disruption has prompted protests from Astana, warnings from Washington, and calls for Ukraine to avoid infrastructure and shipping linked to Kazakhstan.
Timeline
February 17, 2025
Kropotkinskaya Pumping Station Struck
2026
Seven drones carrying explosives and metal fragments attacked CPC’s Kropotkinskaya pumping station in Russia’s Krasnodar region. CPC reported serious damage and took the station out of operation, although the pipeline continued working through a bypass. The facility was the largest CPC pumping station on Russian territory.
(Reuters)
February 18, 2025
Ukraine Confirms the Attack
2026
Ukraine’s General Staff confirmed that its forces had struck Kropotkinskaya. Russian officials said pipeline flows had fallen, while Kazakhstan’s Energy Ministry said there were no restrictions on accepting Kazakh crude and that exports remained stable.
(The Times of Central Asia)

Astana Opens Diplomatic Contacts
2026

(Kursiv)

Exports Remain on Schedule
2026
Kazakhstan said CPC exports were continuing according to schedule despite the damage. Energy Minister Almasadam Satkaliyev said crude was bypassing Kropotkinskaya and that storage capacity remained sufficient. Repairs were expected to take about two months.
(Reuters)

2026

2026

August 14-18, 2026
Kazakhstan’s Other Russian Export Routes Come Under Pressure
2026
On August 14, a drone attack halted crude loadings at the Sheskharis terminal at Novorossiysk, which handles Kazakhstan’s KEBCO crude as well as Russian oil. The same day, a Ukrainian drone attack caused a fire at Ust-Luga on Russia’s Baltic coast, although Russian authorities said the damage was quickly cleared. Operations at Sheskharis resumed on August 16, with Kazakh crude among the first cargoes loaded. On August 18, at least two Kazakh KEBCO cargoes scheduled for Ust-Luga were redirected to Novorossiysk, freeing about 100,000 barrels per day of Baltic export capacity for Russian crude. The shift further concentrated Kazakhstan’s Russia-routed exports on the Black Sea even as Astana seeks to expand alternatives.
(The Times of Central Asia)
August 25, 2026
Oil Production Forecast Cut
2026
Energy Minister Yerlan Akkenzhenov said Kazakhstan had reduced its 2026 oil-production forecast from 98 million to 96 million tonnes following disruptions at CPC. He attributed about 3.5 million tonnes of lost production to attacks, with postponed field maintenance partly offsetting the shortfall. The minister also backed Baku–Supsa as an alternative export route, but said direct negotiations with Azerbaijan had not yet begun.

2026
A drone incident near CPC’s Black Sea terminal temporarily halted tanker loading at SPM-2 and SPM-3.
