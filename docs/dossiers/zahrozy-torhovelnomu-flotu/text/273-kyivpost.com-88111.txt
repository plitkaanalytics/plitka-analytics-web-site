Bulgarians Vote for President as Black Sea Drone Strikes Escalate
In brief:
Bulgarians are voting in presidential elections on Sunday, Oct. 25, amid regional security strains following fatal drone strikes on commercial cargo ships off Bulgaria’s Black Sea coast. Acting President Iliana Iotova leads polling at 41 percent over pro-Western former caretaker prime minister Andrey Gyurov at 28 percent, with a runoff set for Nov. 1 if no candidate secures an absolute majority. The vote unfolds as Sofia requests NATO air and naval assets to secure commercial maritime lanes, while both leading contenders present contrasting foreign policies regarding support for Ukraine and transatlantic security.

Bulgarians are voting in presidential elections on Sunday as Russia’s war in Ukraine spreads to the waters off Bulgaria’s Black Sea coast.
Polls place acting President Iliana Iotova, 61, and ex-caretaker prime minister Andrey Gyurov, 50, as leaders in a race of 27 candidates.

While the presidency is largely ceremonial, Sunday’s vote has taken on added significance after a series of drone attacks on merchant ships off Bulgaria’s Black Sea coast that likely killed some two-dozen international seafarers.
The strikes, which sparked international condemnation and concerns about the growing reach of the war in Ukraine, prompted the government to seek allied assistance to bolster maritime security.
Bulgaria has stopped short of directly accusing Russia of carrying out the attacks, but Prime Minister Rumen Radev said their outcome “benefits the Russian Federation”.
Advertisement
Both leading presidential candidates condemned the strikes, but they differ over the broader war.
Gyurov, who served as caretaker prime minister this year, is supported by pro-Western, reformist parties and has long expressed support for Ukraine. He signed a security cooperation agreement with Kyiv during his time as premier.
“I want Bulgaria to have a voice of its own in Europe. A reliable NATO ally that defends its interests through preparedness, strong diplomacy and a clear position,” he said at a rally on Thursday.

Iotova, formerly vice president, became acting president in January after Radev resigned to enter the parliamentary race, which he won on what many political analysts considered a Kremlin-friendly ticket. Radev supports Iotova and they both call for a diplomatic end to the war.
A victory for Iotova would make her Bulgaria’s first elected female president.
“My opponents argue that there must be a war of attrition.” she told a rally on Thursday. “The truth is, we are all the ones who are most exhausted.”

Polls by Alpha Research give Iotova 41% of the vote and Gyurov 28%. If no candidate wins a majority, a runoff vote will be held on Nov. 1.
Voting started at 7 a.m. local time and polls close at 8 p.m.
