            Kropotkinskaya Pumping Station Struck 2026
Seven drones carrying explosives and metal fragments attacked CPC’s Kropotkinskaya pumping station in Russia’s Krasnodar region. CPC reported serious damage and took the station out of operation, although the pipeline continued working through a bypass. The facility was the largest CPC pumping station on Russian territory.                 July 22, 2026            
            
            Production Cuts Reach Tengiz 2026
Kazakhstan’s oil and gas condensate output fell to 1.63 million barrels per day from a July average of 2.07 million. Tengiz production dropped by 56%, from about 925,000 barrels per day to 406,000, as producers reduced output to prevent their storage tanks from filling.

Ukraine’s ambassador to Kazakhstan, Victor Mayko, said there was no evidence that Ukraine had carried out the July 17 and 19 attacks. He said Kazakhstan’s infrastructure and economic interests were not Ukrainian military targets. [(The Times of Central Asia)](https://timesca.com/ukraine-denies-role-in-attacks-on-tankers-loading-kazakh-oil-statement-in-full/)

                August 12, 2026            
            
            Vance Personally Pressed Zelensky on CPC Strikes 2026
The Financial Times reported that U.S. Vice President JD Vance personally asked Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky during a July 31 call to halt attacks on tankers serving the CPC terminal. Kyiv reportedly agreed not to target CPC infrastructure or non-Russian vessels unless they were sanctioned by Ukraine or carrying Russian cargo. A U.S. official described CPC as “a vital conduit of Kazakhstan-origin energy for European markets.” Ukraine has not struck tankers near the terminal since the call.

                August 14-18, 2026            
            
            Kazakhstan’s Other Russian Export Routes Come Under Pressure 2026
On August 14, a drone attack halted crude loadings at the Sheskharis terminal at Novorossiysk, which handles Kazakhstan’s KEBCO crude as well as Russian oil. The same day, a Ukrainian drone attack caused a fire at Ust-Luga on Russia’s Baltic coast, although Russian authorities said the damage was quickly cleared. Operations at Sheskharis resumed on August 16, with Kazakh crude among the first cargoes loaded. On August 18, at least two Kazakh KEBCO cargoes scheduled for Ust-Luga were redirected to Novorossiysk, freeing about 100,000 barrels per day of Baltic export capacity for Russian crude. The shift further concentrated Kazakhstan’s Russia-routed exports on the Black Sea even as Astana seeks to expand alternatives.

 [(The Times of Central Asia)](https://timesca.com/push-for-kazakhstan-oil-exports-diversification-as-cpc-disruptions-expose-capacity-gap/)

                August 25, 2026            
            
            Oil Production Forecast Cut 2026
Energy Minister Yerlan Akkenzhenov said Kazakhstan had reduced its 2026 oil-production forecast from 98 million to 96 million tonnes following disruptions at CPC. He attributed about 3.5 million tonnes of lost production to attacks, with postponed field maintenance partly offsetting the shortfall. The minister also backed Baku–Supsa as an alternative export route, but said direct negotiations with Azerbaijan had not yet begun.

            Drone Incident Briefly Interrupts CPC Loading 2026
A drone incident near CPC’s Black Sea terminal temporarily halted tanker loading at SPM-2 and SPM-3. Acceptance of crude from Kazakh shippers was not restricted.
