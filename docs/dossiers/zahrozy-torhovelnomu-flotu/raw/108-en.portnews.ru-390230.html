IAA PortNews: Russian seaports Q1 2026 cargo volume edges up to more than 210 million tonnes

Container traffic at the country’s seaports fell 4.4%, reaching 1.32 million TEUs.

Total freight volume handled at Russian seaports in the first quarter of 2026 exceeded 210 million metric tons, which is a 0.6% gain from the same three month period a year earlier, JSC Mortcenter’s data showed.

According the Moscow based IT company, there was a double-digits growth the Caspian, Arctic, and Far Eastern basins ports in January-March this year: 24.1-, 12.2%, and 10.6%, respectively. Two basins reported a decline in handled volumes: the Azov-Black Sea (-5.8%) and the Baltic (-5.6%).

Exports totaled 166.67 million tonnes (+1.1%), while coastal cargo volume jumped 20.7% reaching 18.97 million tonnes. Transit volumes decreased by 19.8% to 14.49 million tonnes and imports declined 1.8% to 9.92 million tonnes.

Handling of dry cargo in the first quarter rose 3.5% to 102.49 million tonnes. Container throughput in tonnes was 98% of the previous year's level while the volume in twenty-foot units dropped 4.4% to 1,316,036 TEUs.

In the reported period, the volume of liquid bulk cargo handled at the country’s seaports decreased by 2% to 107.57 million tonnes, while the volume in the segment of liquefied gas increased.

The Association of Commercial Sea Ports (ACSP) data for dry cargo segment showed that coal volume rose 3% reaching 45.4 million tonnes, grain — by 39.4% to 13.1 million tonnes, ore — by 17.4% to 3.4 million tonnes, and ferry cargo — by 13.8% to 2.1 million tonnes. Handling of containerized cargo in January-March reached 13.4 million tonnes (-2%), mineral fertilizers — 11.6 million tonnes (-3.8%), and ferrous metals — 5.6 million tonnes (-4.8%).

In the liquid bulk segment the seaports handled 64.9 million tonnes of crude oil (-0.2%), 29.7 million tonnes of oil products (-8.6%), 10.8 million tonnes of liquefied gas (+11.7%), and 1.4 million tonnes of food products (-4.1%).

Freight traffic at the Arctic Basin seaports in the first quarter totaled 24.2 million tonnes. The volume includes 4.8 million tonnes of dry bulk (-13.1%) and 19.4 million tonnes of liquid bulk cargo (+21.1%). Port of Murmansk handled 13.8 million tonnes (+10.3%), Port Sabetta — 8.1 million tonnes (+13.6%), Port of Arkhangelsk — 0.4 million tonnes (-26.2%). Cargo throughput at Port of Varandey soared 61.6% reaching 1.4 million tonnes.

The Baltic Basin seaports cargo throughput totaled 64.3 million tonnes. The volume includes 28.4 million tonnes of dry bulk (-10.4%) and 35.9 million tonnes of liquid bulk cargo (-1.5%). Port of Ust-Luga Port handled 30 million tonnes (-6.8%), Port Primorsk — 17 million tonnes (+3.7%), Greater Port of St. Petersburg — 12.7 million tonnes (-11.3%), and Port Vysotsk — 2 million tonnes (-30.9%).

The Azov-Black Sea basin seaports handled in the reporting period 59.3 million tonnes of different cargo (-5.8%). The three-month volume includes 29.2 million tonnes of dry bulk (+11.7%) and 30.1 million tonnes of liquid bulk cargo (-18.3%). The Port of Novorossiysk volume decreased by 6.5% to 36.8 million tonnes, Port of Tuapse – by 15.4% to 4.5 million tonnes, Port Kavkaz — by 24.1% to 2.6 million tonnes, Port of Rostov-on-Don – by 28.8% to 2.1 million tonnes. At the same time, Port of Taman handled 9.6 million tonnes of different cargo, which represents a 23.2-percent growth year-on-year. 

The Caspian basin seaports three-month volume rose by 24.1% reaching 2.1 million tonnes of different cargo. This includes 1.3 million tonnes of dry cargo (+23.5%), 0.9 million tonnes of liquid cargo (+25.0%). Handling of freight at the Port of Astrakhan totaled 1.1 million tonnes (+16.9%), at the Port of Makhachkala — 1.1 million tonnes (+47.5%).

Cargo throughput at the Far East basin seaports in January-March rose 10.6%, reaching 60.2 million tonnes. The volume includes 38.8 million tonne of dry cargo (+12.2%), and 21.4 million tonnes of liquid bulk cargo (+7.8%). Vostochny Port handled 21.7 million tonnes (+10%), Port Vanino – 12.6 million tonnes (+54.5%), Port of Vladivostok – 8.3 million tonnes (-12.8%), Port of Nakhodka – 7.5 million tonnes (+3.8%), and the specialized Port Prigorodnoye – 3.9 million tonnes (+6.3%).
