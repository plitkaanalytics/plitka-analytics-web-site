**FP-1** is a Ukrainian long-range, [one-way attack drone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-way_attack_drone) developed and produced by the defence technology company [Fire Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_Point_(Ukrainian_firm)). Introduced in late 2024 in response to the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Ukrainian_war_(2022–present)), the FP-1 is designed for deep-strike missions, capable of delivering a [warhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warhead) over distances up to 1,600 km. It has been deployed by the [Armed Forces of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_Ukraine) to target high-value military and logistical sites.[[1]](#cite_note-PBS-1)[[2]](#cite_note-United24-2)[[3]](#cite_note-Defender-3)

## Development

Conceived in mid-2022 by a team of former aerospace engineers and defence analysts, the FP-1 programme aimed to produce a low-cost, high-performance [one-way attack drone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-way_attack_drone). Initial operational prototypes of the FP-1 drone flew in early 2024, demonstrating a loitering munition delivery system. [4] Full-scale production commenced in October 2024 at covert facilities near Kyiv.


[[2]](#cite_note-United24-2)## Design

The FP-1 features a slender [fuselage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuselage) with a two-cylinder engine driving a propeller, a solid rocket booster for launching, and fixed straight wings.[[4]](#cite_note-DefenseUA-4) [5] It carries a modular warhead weighing between 60 kg and 120 kg, selectable for fragmentation or shaped-charge effects. The airframe employs 

[radar-absorbent materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar-absorbent_material)to reduce detectability. Navigation relies on

[inertial systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertial_navigation)augmented by satellite guidance, with

[electronic counter-countermeasure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_counter-countermeasure)capabilities to resist jamming.


[[4]](#cite_note-DefenseUA-4)## Operational history

By mid-2025, Fire Point had increased production from 30 to over 100 FP-1 units per day, reporting total manufacture of more than 5,000 drones. The UAV has been credited with striking ammunition depots, command posts, and air defence batteries at ranges exceeding 1,200 km. 
Analysts attribute Ukraine's ability to disrupt rear-area [logistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistics) to the FP-1's [cost-effectiveness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cost-effectiveness) – estimated at US$55,000 per unit versus US$200,000 for comparable systems.[[1]](#cite_note-PBS-1) [3]
Recent capability expansions include carrying two deployable FPV drones on the wings.

On 12 May 2026, a Ukrainian FP-1/2 attacked an air defence system, in Crimea, using unguided missiles for the first time. According to the Russian Telegram channel, footage showed it launching one of four missiles attached to its wings.[[6]](#cite_note-6)

On 6 July 2026, Ukrainian drones, reported to be FP-1, travelled over 2,500 km and hit Russia's [Omsk refinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omsk_refinery), the largest oil refinery in Russia.[[7]](#cite_note-7)
