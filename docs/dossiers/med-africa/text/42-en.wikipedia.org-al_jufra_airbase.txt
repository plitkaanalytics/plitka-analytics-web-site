**Al Jufra Airbase** ([ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO_airport_code): **HL69**) is a [Libyan Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Air_Force) base in Al Jufra , 9 kilometres (5.6 mi) northwest of [Waddan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waddan,_Libya). It was originally used by the Libyan Arab Air Force during the [Gaddafi-era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Libya_under_Muammar_Gaddafi) from 1969 to 2011.

The runway length does not include 198 metres (650 ft) paved overruns on each end.

## History

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Al_Jufra_Airbase&action=edit§ion=1)]

Prior to the [First Libyan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Civil_War_(2011)), during the 1980s, the No. 1025 Squadron operated [Mig-25PDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan-Gurevich_MiG-25) at the Al Jufra-Hun Airbase for defense of the north coast. [TU-22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TU-22), [G.222](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G.222), and [An-26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An-26) were based at Jufra, parked on open pavement, sand berm shielded pads, and in aircraft shelters. [3] Considering the positioning of many of the aircraft visible in the satellite imagery, and what is known of the maintenance record of the 

[Libyan Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Air_Force_(1951-2011)), the operational inventory of combat aircraft based at Jufra in 2011 can be assumed to be quite low. The G.222 is believed to have not flown for more than a decade.

## No-Fly Zone enforcement

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Al_Jufra_Airbase&action=edit§ion=2)]

The airbase was bombed on 24 March 2011 by French aircraft, in accordance with the UN-backed enforcement of the No-Fly Zone over Libya. [4] Over two and a half months later, on 13 June, the area was again struck by NATO forces.
