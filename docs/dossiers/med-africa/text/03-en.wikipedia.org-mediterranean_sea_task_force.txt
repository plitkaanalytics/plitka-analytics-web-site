The **permanent task force of the Russian Navy in the Mediterranean Sea** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Постоянное оперативное соединение Военно-морского флота Российской Федерации в Средиземном море) is a [task force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Task_force) of the [Russian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Navy) responsible for [projecting Russian power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_projection) in the [Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East) through the [Mediterranean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Sea).

The [Soviet Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Navy)'s [5th Operational Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_Operational_Squadron) had fulfilled a similar role from its formation in 1967 to its deactivation on 31 December 1992. [2]  The present permanent task force was established in 2013.


[[3]](#cite_note-3)In September 2013, the [Russian Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_(Russia)) re-established a fleet in the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean) using a combination of ships from the [Black Sea Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Fleet) and [Northern Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Fleet) stationed in [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria). [1] Historically, it has comprised up to 15 warships and auxiliary vessels

with vessels from the

[[4]](#cite_note-4)[Baltic Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Fleet)also providing contingents for operations in the region.

However, with the loss of regular use of Russian naval facilities in Syria, during 2025, these numbers were significantly lower by early 2026.

[[5]](#cite_note-5)

[[6]](#cite_note-6)In mid-2026 it was reported that there were no major Russian warships currently in the Mediterranean. [7] Nevertheless, in August 2026 it was announced that an agreement had been signed between Russia and Syria governing future Russian use of the bases

although Russia also seemed increasingly to be looking at the ports of

[[8]](#cite_note-8)[Tobruk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobruk)in

[Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya)as well as

[Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algiers)as more consistent ports of call.

### Role in the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Mediterranean_Sea_Task_Force&action=edit§ion=12)]

In conjunction with a build-up of Russian forces around Ukraine and in Belarus, Russia began to reinforce the task force in the Mediterranean toward the end of 2021. The principal movements involved the deployment of two [Slava-class cruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slava-class_cruiser) - [ Marshal Ustinov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_cruiser_Marshal_Ustinov) from the 

[Northern Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Fleet)and

[from the](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_cruiser_Varyag_(1983))

*Varyag*[Pacific Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Fleet_(Russia))- to the Mediterranean, together with additional escorts.


[[123]](#cite_note-123)At the same time, the submarine

[[124]](#cite_note-124)[returned to the Black Sea from the Mediterranean just prior to the outbreak of hostilities.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_submarine_B-237)

*Rostov Na Donu*The movements strengthened Russian anti-surface group capabilities in the Mediterranean prior to the outbreak of war. Simultaneously,

[[125]](#cite_note-125)[Bastion-P anti-ship missile batteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastion_missile)were installed at the Russian base in Syria to improve its defensive capabilities.


[[126]](#cite_note-126)On 28 February 2022, four days after the outbreak of hostilities, [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey) indicated that it was closing the [Dardanelles Straits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dardanelles) to all foreign warships for the duration of the conflict. Turkish Foreign Minister, [Mevlüt Çavuşoğlu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mevlüt_Çavuşoğlu), argued that the move was consistent with terms of the [Montreaux Convention of 1936](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreaux_Convention). An exception would be allowed for Russian ships returning from the Mediterranean to Black Sea bases where they were registered. The move limited Russia's ability to reinforce its naval units in the Mediterranean from the Black Sea though it also effectively confined NATO naval forces to the Mediterranean.[[127]](#cite_note-127)[[128]](#cite_note-128)

### 2023

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Mediterranean_Sea_Task_Force&action=edit§ion=13)]

The Russian missile corvette [Mercury 734](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_734), which is the fifth ship in [Project 20380](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_20380) built at the [Severnaya shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severnaya_shipyard), visited the [Port of Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Algiers?action=edit&redlink=1) in August 2023. This visit heralded increased collaboration between governments. Russia hopes to invest the [Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel) region by this means.[[129]](#cite_note-at1-129)

Russia began a gradual withdrawal of its main warships from the Mediterranean since summer 2022. As of 15 October 2023, the number of Russian warships was six in October 2023. As of then, it included two missile corvettes, one anti-submarine warfare boat, one reconnaissance ship, one supply tanker and one floating shipyard. One analyst concluded that "Russia no longer considers the prospect of the group’s passing through the Turkish Straits realistic".[[130]](#cite_note-bsn23-130)

[[132]](#cite_note-132)### Status as of 2025/26

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Mediterranean_Sea_Task_Force&action=edit§ion=15)]

As of mid-2025, the loss of the [Russian base at the port of Tartus in Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartus_naval_base), as a result of the [Fall of the Assad regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Assad_regime) in late 2024, has significantly impacted the ability of the Russian navy to maintain an effective presence in the Mediterranean region. The closure of the [Turkish Straits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Straits) as a result of the [Russo-Ukraine War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Ukraine_War) has also negatively impacted the ability of the Russian navy to support its naval forces from its ports in the [Black Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea). This has compelled the Russian navy to sustain its forces by drawing on units based in its [Baltic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Fleet) and [Northern fleets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Fleet).

Whereas previously the Russian naval task force in the Mediterranean comprised up to fifteen warships and auxiliaries, as of mid-2025 the only major units operating in the region were the [Project 636.3 submarine  Novorossiysk (B-261)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilo-class_submarine), the 

[frigate Admiral Grigorovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_frigate_Admiral_Grigorovich)


[[133]](#cite_note-133)and one

[[134]](#cite_note-134)[Steregushchiy-class multi-role corvette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steregushchiy-class_corvette).

Russia's naval presence in the Mediterranean reportedly suffered a further setback in September when

[[135]](#cite_note-135)*Novorossiysk*sustained serious damage as the result of a fuel system malfunction.

As of mid-2025, Russia and Syria were reportedly engaged in negotiations about the future of Russian naval and air facilities in the country though it remained unclear how extensive Russia's military presence in Syria would be going forward.

[[136]](#cite_note-136)

[[137]](#cite_note-137)With the October surface-return of *Novorossiysk* to the Baltic due to mechanical failure and a lack of maintenance facilities in the Mediterranean, *Naval News* reported that the problem for Russia is not merely a single diesel-electric submarine, but rather "since losing its Syrian base at Tartus in 2024 and facing restrictions on movement through the [Bosporus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosporus), Russia’s Mediterranean task force has largely collapsed."[[138]](#cite_note-navnews20251015-138)

As of March 2026, the Russian naval presence in the Mediterranean was limited to the  [Corvette Stoikiy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_corvette_Stoikiy) and the submarine *Krasnodar* which herself departed the Mediterranean later in the month. Some limited Russian use of the Tartus facility appeared to have been restored. In early 2026, *Stoykiy* was reported to be using the facility more regularly, including wharfs previously reserved for the Russian Mediterranean Flotilla.[[139]](#cite_note-139)

However, Russia also seemed increasingly to be looking at the ports of [Tobruk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobruk) in [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya) as well as [Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algiers) as more consistent ports of call. [140] In mid-2026, it was reported that there were no Russian warships currently in the Mediterranean.
