* Aleksandr Shabalin* (

[Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Александр Шабалин) is a

[Ropucha-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ropucha-class_landing_ship)

[landing ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_ship)of the

[Russian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Navy)and part of the

[Baltic Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Fleet).

Named after the Soviet naval officer and twice-[Hero of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero_of_the_Soviet_Union) [Aleksandr Shabalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Shabalin), the ship was built in [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_People's_Republic) and [launched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceremonial_ship_launching) in 1985.  She was named * BDK-60* (

[Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): БДК-60) for Russian: Большой десантный корабль, romanized:

*Bolshoy desantnyi korabl'*, lit. 'large landing ship', from her construction until being renamed

*Aleksandr Shabalin*in 1986. She is one of the subtype of the Ropucha-class landing ships, designated Project 775/II by the Russian Navy.

## Construction and commissioning

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Russian_landing_ship_Aleksandr_Shabalin&action=edit§ion=1)]

*Aleksandr Shabalin* was built as *BDK-60* by [Stocznia Północna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stocznia_Północna), part of [Gdańsk Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gdańsk_Shipyard), in [Gdańsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gdańsk), in what was then the [Polish People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_People's_Republic).  She was laid down in 1985, and launched on 11 June 1985. She was commissioned into the [Soviet Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Navy) on 31 December 1985 as part of its [Baltic Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Fleet), [homeported](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeport) in [Baltiysk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltiysk), and with the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) in late December 1991, she went on to serve in the Russian Navy.[[1]](#cite_note-flot-1)

## Career

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Russian_landing_ship_Aleksandr_Shabalin&action=edit§ion=2)]

In service since 1985 as *BDK-60*, she was renamed *Aleksandr Shabalin* on 12 November 1986.[[2]](#cite_note-rus-2)*Aleksandr Shabalin* was undergoing a refit at the [Yantar Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yantar_Shipyard) in [Kaliningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaliningrad), when a fire broke out in the engine room on 10 April 2009, subsequently spreading to the refrigeration compartment and burnt out 5 square meters. Twelve fire engines and 42 firefighters successfully extinguished the fire. [1]  Also that month a greeting card with nine compressed hashish plates glued to it was found on board during a check of letters, leading to the filling of a criminal case.


[[1]](#cite_note-flot-1)The ship entered the Yantar Shipyard again on 16 April 2012 for repairs, but was back at sea for a two-week voyage in early July, which included a visit to [Zeebrugge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeebrugge) for Belgian Navy Day. [3] She was dispatched the following year to the Mediterranean as part of the 

[Syrian Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Express), transporting personnel and equipment between

[Black Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea)ports and the

[Russian naval facility in Tartus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartus_naval_base), going on to support the

[Russian intervention in the Syrian civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_intervention_in_the_Syrian_civil_war). She entered the naval base at

[Novorossiysk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novorossiysk)on 21 January and 27 March 2013, and made return visits to the Mediterranean in 2014 and 2015.
