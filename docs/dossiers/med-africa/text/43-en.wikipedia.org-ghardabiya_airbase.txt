**Ghardabiya Airbase** ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Air_Transport_Association_airport_code): **SRX**, [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Civil_Aviation_Organization_airport_code): **HLGD**) is a dual-function [airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbase) for the [Libyan Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Air_Force_(1951-2011)) 15 kilometres (9.3 mi) south of the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Sea) coastal city of [Sirte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirte). [4] It also incorporates an airport for civilian use.


[[5]](#cite_note-5)All runways have 305 metres (1,001 ft) [displaced thresholds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Displaced_threshold).

The Sirte [non-directional beacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-directional_beacon) (Ident: **SRT**) is located 7.8 nautical miles (14.4 km) north of the airport, in the city. The Sirte [VOR-DME](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VOR-DME) (Ident: **SRT**) is located on the field.[[6]](#cite_note-6)[[7]](#cite_note-7)

## Military use

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Ghardabiya_Airbase&action=edit§ion=1)]

It is the home base of 1124th Bomber Squadron and 1st Fighter-Bomber Squadron which operate the [Su-22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-17) aircraft. [8] The base also operates the 

[Aero L-39 Albatros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aero_L-39_Albatros)for counterinsurgency missions.

During the [first Gulf of Sidra Incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Sidra_incident_(1981)), two Libyan Su-22s took off from this airbase and attacked two [U.S. Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy) [F-14 Tomcats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grumman_F-14_Tomcat).

## Civilian use

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Ghardabiya_Airbase&action=edit§ion=2)]

Due to the [First Libyan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Libyan_Civil_War) of 2011, all flights were suspended; however, since the end of the conflict, [Libyan Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Airlines) has resumed service. It is unknown if [Air Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Libya) has resumed services as well.

## First Libyan Civil War (2011)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Ghardabiya_Airbase&action=edit§ion=3)]

It was targeted by [United States Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force) [B-2 Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman_B-2_Spirit) stealth bombers equipped with [precision-guided munitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision-guided_munition) (PGMs) during the [First Libyan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Libyan_Civil_War) on 20 and 21 March 2011. [9] The 

[United States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy)also participated in the attack to deny the Libyan Air Force operational capability with the use of

[Tomahawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomahawk_(missile_family))

[cruise missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruise_missile).


[[10]](#cite_note-10)

[[11]](#cite_note-11)## Second Libyan Civil War (2014–2020)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Ghardabiya_Airbase&action=edit§ion=4)]

In May 2015, during the [Second Libyan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Libyan_Civil_War), [ISIL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State_in_Libya) militants took control of the airport only weeks after [seizing full control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Sirte_(2015)) of nearby Sirte. [12] On 4 June 2016, Pro-Libyan government fighters stated that they had 

[captured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Sirte_(2016))the airbase from ISIL.


[[13]](#cite_note-13)In January 2020, Libyan forces loyal to the eastern-based commander [Khalifa Haftar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalifa_Haftar) said they had [taken control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Sirte_(2020)) of the strategic coastal city of Sirte, including al-Ghardabiya airbase.[[14]](#cite_note-14)
