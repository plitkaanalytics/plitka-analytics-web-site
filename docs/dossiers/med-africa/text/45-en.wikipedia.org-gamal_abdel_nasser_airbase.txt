**Gamal Abdel Nasser Airbase** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): قاعدة جمال عبد الناصر الجوية, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Qāʿida Ǧamāl ʿAbd an-Nāṣir Ǧawwiyya*) is a [Libyan Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Air_Force_(1951–2011)) ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): القوات الجوية الليبية, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *al-Quwwāt al-Ǧawwiyya al-Lībiyya*, [Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_languages): **Adwas Alibyan Ujnna**) base, located about 16 km south of [Tobruk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobruk).

Before [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), it had been an [Italian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Air_Force) airfield. A number of the former Italian buildings were seen remaining in 2003, during a courtesy visit by former RAF personnel, at which time no military aircraft were evident.

Prior to 31 March 1970, the airfield was known as **Royal Air Force Station El Adem** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): العدم, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *al-ʿAdam* after the nearby settlement [al Adm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Adm)), and used by the RAF primarily as a staging post. [1] This was for RAF and civil aircraft transiting from bases in Europe to others further East.

## World War II

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Gamal_Abdel_Nasser_Airbase&action=edit§ion=1)]

The airfield saw heavy use during the [Western Desert Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Desert_Campaign) of 1941–42. [No. 211 Group RAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._211_Group_RAF) made its headquarters at the airfield for several months.

The airfield was largely reconstructed in 1942 by the [Royal Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Air_Force) (RAF) and brought into operational use again on 12 December 1942. It was used during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) by the RAF and the [United States Army Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Air_Forces) (USAAF) during the [North African Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_African_Campaign) against German and Italian forces.

## Postwar to British departure

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Gamal_Abdel_Nasser_Airbase&action=edit§ion=2)]

Royal Air Force Station El Adem was the fuel stop for the [BOAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BOAC) aircraft carrying the new [Queen Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_II) on her flight from [Entebbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entebbe) to London on 7 February 1952.[[3]](#cite_note-3)

During the first half of 1960, a high-speed low-altitude flying effects evaluation flight, the Swifter Flight, was established at the station. [4] Mainly flying the 

[English Electric Canberra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Electric_Canberra), the flight provided data for use in the

[TSR.2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TSR.2)programme as well as more general data about such flight profiles. The aircraft had to be specially strengthened.

No. 1564 (Helicopter) Flight was re-formed at El Adem on 14 August 1963, flying [Bristol Sycamores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Sycamore) and [Westland Whirlwinds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westland_Whirlwind_(helicopter)), from an element of [103 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._103_Squadron_RAF), disbanding on 31 December 1966 at El Adem.[[5]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTELake199995-5) [6] It was  re-formed at El Adem again on 1 May 1969, from 'D' Flight of 

[202 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._202_Squadron_RAF), only to be disbanded again on 31 March 1970 on

[Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus).


[[5]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTELake199995-5)

[[6]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTESturtivantHamlin2007127-6)As a group captain, RAF officer [Peter Terry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Terry) was station commander of El Adem, from 1969 to 1970. He was there in September 1969 when [Colonel Gaddafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonel_Gaddafi) overthrew King [Idris of Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idris_of_Libya) in a coup. Terry oversaw withdrawal of British troops from El Adem and [Tobruk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobruk) in March 1970. He went on to achieve the rank of [air chief marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_chief_marshal).

In 1994, the remaining wreckage of * Lady Be Good*, a USAAF 

[Consolidated B-24 Liberator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consolidated_B-24_Liberator)heavy bomber that crashed-landed deep in the Libyan desert during WWII in 1943, was brought to the air base by a local Libyan team led by Dr. Fadel Ali Mohammed (tasked with recovering the plane wreck) for storage and safekeeping. The remnants of the aircraft still remain there.

Units present 1950s:

- [No. 249 Squadron RAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._249_Squadron_RAF)(11 March – 3 May 1957)
- Swifter Trials Flight (January–July 1960) - high speed flight evaluation unit[[7]](#cite_note-7)
- No 1564 Flight (1 May 1969 – 31 March 1970)
- [No. 1 Squadron RAF Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._1_Squadron_RAF_Regiment)

## Current use

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Gamal_Abdel_Nasser_Airbase&action=edit§ion=3)]

The airbase is named after the Egyptian revolutionary [Gamal Abdel Nasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamal_Abdel_Nasser), who served as President of Egypt. In 2013, the airport was officially reopened as [Tobruk International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobruk_Airport), with flights to [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Nouzha_Airport), [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt).[[8]](#cite_note-8)[[9]](#cite_note-9)
