Coast Guard Seizes Russian-flagged Tanker in North Atlantic, Second Tanker in Caribbean
Mallory Shelbourne and Sam LaGrone
January 7, 2026 10:57 AM - Updated: January 7, 2026 3:25 PM

A US Coast Guardsman aboard USCGC Munro (WMSL-755) observes MT Bella I in the North Atlantic in an undated photo. US European Command photo
This story has been updated to include information from the U.K. Ministry of Defense and the U.S. Navy.

A U.S. Coast Guard team and additional federal law enforcement, with support from the U.S. military, seized a Russian-flagged oiler tanker that the U.S. says violated American sanctions, a U.S. official confirmed Wednesday to USNI News, coinciding with the seizure of a second tanker in the Caribbean.

The U.S. seized MT Bella I on Wednesday in the Atlantic between Iceland and the United Kingdom in an operation that paired Department of Homeland Security assets with U.S. special operations forces, USNI News has learned.

“The vessel was seized in the North Atlantic pursuant to a warrant issued by a U.S. federal court after being tracked by USCGC Munro,” reads a U.S. European Command statement shared to social media website X.

Coast Guard Cutter Munro (WMSL-755) is a Legend-class national security cutter. U.S. officials confirmed to USNI News Navy guided-missile destroyers USS Paul Ignatius (DDG-117) and USS Bulkeley (DDG-84) were operating in the Atlantic but did not say if they were involved in the operation. Both ships are based at Naval Station Rota, Spain.

“The blockade of sanctioned and illicit Venezuelan oil remains in FULL EFFECT — anywhere in the world,” Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth wrote on X.

Bella I, which the U.S. sanctioned for transporting Iranian oil, has evaded a U.S. blockade of Venezuelan oil exports for more than two weeks. The empty tanker was believed to be heading to Venezuela to load up on crude oil when the U.S. blockade went into effect, according to Sal Mercogliano, a former U.S. Military Sealift Command mariner and current history professor at Campbell University.

A helicopter that appears to be an MH-6 Little Bird approaching MT Bella I on Jan. 7, 2026. Photo via RT
Initially flagged as a Guyanese ship, Bella I changed its registration to Russia and began heading for the North Atlantic. The ship was renamed Marinera while underway and picked up a Russian Navy escort that included at least one attack submarine, The Wall Street Journal reported Tuesday.

“The U.S. doesn’t view the Russian flag as authentic. I don’t think they buy the registry,” Mercogliano said. “It shows the Russians can’t do a damn thing about it. Submarines aren’t good for escorting ships.”

Photos released from Russian state media and republished by the BBC show what appear to be MH-6 Little Bird helicopters approaching a ship identified as Bella I on Wednesday and video of Munro. The MH-6 is almost exclusively used by the U.S. Army’s 160th Special Operations Aviation Regiment. A Pentagon spokesperson did not confirm the unit when reached by USNI News.

Over the last several days, plane spotters have tracked an influx of U.S. Air Force transport planes flying to the U.K., with some open source intelligence analysts claiming 160th forces were aboard. It’s unclear which platform the mission to take Bella I originated from. In the Caribbean, Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem said Wednesday a Coast Guard team seized Panamanian tanker MT Sophia and released a video of a Coast Guard law enforcement team fast roping from a Navy MH-60S helicopter onto the deck of the tanker and moving to the bridge of the ship. Sophia looks fully loaded, Mercogliano told USNI News.

On Dec. 10, a Coast Guard Maritime Security Response Team originating from aircraft carrier USS Gerald Ford (CVN-78) seized tanker MT Skipper off the coast of Venezuela.

The seizures come several days after the U.S. launched an operation into Caracas, Venezuela, that captured the country’s leader, Nicolás Maduro, and his wife, Cilia Flores.

“One of these tankers, Motor Tanker Bella I, has been trying to evade the Coast Guard for weeks, even changing its flag and painting a new name on the hull while being pursued, in a desperate and failed attempt to escape justice,” Noem wrote Wednesday on X. Sophia and Bella I “were either last docked in Venezuela or en route to it,” Noem said.

The U.K. Ministry of Defense on Wednesday said it helped the U.S. with planning and basing ahead of Bella I‘s seizure. The Royal Air Force also aided the U.S. with surveillance and fleet oiler RFA Tideforce (A139) helped the U.S. with the seizure.

“The ship, initially flying a false flag, turned off its transponders while at sea and sought to reflag while being pursued, indicating its nefarious links to global sanctions evasion,” reads a U.K. MoD news release.

“Assessments going back several years that the Bella I has been involved in illegal activity, linked to international terrorism and crime including Hezbollah, and part of the web of rising shadow activity that fuels and finances nefarious activity across the globe.”
