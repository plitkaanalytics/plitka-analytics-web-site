Russian LNG Tanker Arctic Metagaz Ablaze After Drone Attack in Mediterranean

by Bill Giannopoulos
0 0 0

A Russian-flagged liquefied natural gas (LNG) tanker, the Arctic Metagaz, part of Russia’s sanctioned “shadow fleet,” caught fire in the central Mediterranean Sea early Tuesday (March 3, 2026) following what maritime security sources describe as a suspected drone attack. The incident occurred in international waters southeast of Malta and off the coast of Libya, with the vessel reportedly laden with cargo from recent Arctic loadings.

Footage circulating on social media and verified by outlets like Reuters, gCaptain, and Maltese media shows the large tanker (IMO 9243148, built 2003, ~277m long) engulfed in intense flames and smoke after multiple explosions around 4:00 a.m. local time. The ship’s AIS signal was lost shortly after, and no official distress call was initially recorded.

Maritime risk firm EOS Risk Group reported the vessel suffered a drone attack while transiting eastbound toward the Suez Canal after departing Murmansk on February 24. Sources cited by Reuters and others suspect Ukrainian involvement, possibly using naval (maritime) drones, though no official confirmation or evidence has been provided by Kyiv or Moscow. This would align with Ukraine’s ongoing campaign targeting Russian shadow fleet vessels to disrupt sanctioned energy exports.

The fate of the crew was initially unknown amid reports of heavy fires and explosions, raising concerns over potential casualties or environmental risks from an LNG carrier blaze. However, Maltese authorities (Armed Forces of Malta Rescue Coordination Centre) later confirmed all crew members were safe, having abandoned ship and taken to lifeboats before being accounted for.

A German military maritime patrol aircraft from Sigonella, Sicily, was observed circling the area for hours, per Flightradar24 data, while Turkish naval assets were also reportedly nearby. The incident falls outside Malta’s Search and Rescue zone, likely in the Libyan SAR area.

The Arctic Metagaz has been under U.S., UK, and EU sanctions since 2024 for its role in transporting Russian LNG while evading Western restrictions imposed after the 2022 invasion of Ukraine. It is linked to exports from projects like Arctic LNG 2.

No large-scale rescue operation was immediately detailed, though crew evacuation appears successful based on later updates. The blaze highlights escalating risks to shadow fleet shipping amid the Russia-Ukraine war, with previous incidents including a December 2025 Ukrainian drone strike on another tanker (Qendil) in the Mediterranean—the first such attack far from Ukrainian waters.

The situation remains fluid, with potential implications for global LNG flows, insurance rates, and maritime security in the region. No official statements have emerged from Russia’s Novatek, the vessel’s manager, or transport ministry as of publication.

Greek City Times is monitoring developments from maritime trackers, Reuters, gCaptain, Maltese authorities, and other reliable sources—updates expected as investigations continue.
