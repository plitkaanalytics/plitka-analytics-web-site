When Lt. Gen. Abdel Fattah al-Burhan, Sudan’s military ruler, found himself besieged by [rebel forces](https://www.wsj.com/articles/sudan-khartoum-military-what-is-happening-447d40e2?mod=article_inline) in the country’s capital last summer, he called an unlikely ally for help: Ukrainian President [Volodymyr Zelensky](https://www.wsj.com/topics/person/volodymyr-zelensky).

Zelensky had reasons to take the request seriously: Burhan had been quietly supplying Kyiv with weapons since shortly after [Russia](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/russia) invaded Ukraine in 2022, according to Ukrainian and Sudanese military officials. In addition, the [Sudanese rebels](https://www.wsj.com/world/africa/sudan-militia-takes-towns-that-had-been-seen-as-havens-from-war-71a46295?mod=article_inline) were backed by [Russia](https://www.wsj.com/topics/place/russia)’s Wagner paramilitary group, which has also mined gold in the country and used it to fund Moscow’s war in Ukraine.

Within a few weeks of the call, Ukrainian commandos landed in Sudan and began fighting to push the rebel forces out of the capital, Khartoum, according to several Ukrainian soldiers involved in the operation.

The front line in the [war between Ukraine and Russia](https://www.wsj.com/world/russia-ukraine-peace-deal-2022-document-6e12e093?mod=article_inline) now extends into Africa.
