Communication Strategy

and Principles

Current Situation and Problem Statement

In 2021-2022, the efforts of Ukrainian diplomacy led to a significant increase in fruitful cooperation between
Ukraine and African countries. In January 2022, for the first time ever, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of
Ukraine adopted a Strategy for the Development of Relations between Ukraine and African States.A significant
step in strengthening interaction between Ukraine and African countries was the appointment of a Special
Representative of Ukraine for the Middle East and Africa in July 2022.

The intensification of relations is also reflected in the growing number and substance of bilateral and multilateral
cooperation formats. In particular, from February 2022 to February 2024, the President of Ukraine held more
than 30 meetings with leaders of African countries and pan-African organizations. The state leadership made a
strategic decision to increase Ukraine’s presence on the African continent, and in December 2022, announced
the opening of 10 new Ukrainian embassies across Africa. During 2022-2023, the Minister of Foreign Affairs,
as part of the New Horizons strategy, undertook four extensive diplomatic tours of Africa, visiting 12 countries
across the continent: Senegal, Côte d’Ivoire, Ghana, Kenya, Morocco, Ethiopia, Mozambique, Rwanda, Nigeria,
Equatorial Guinea, Liberia, and South Africa. Most of these visits were made by Ukraine’s Foreign Minister for
the first time in the history of diplomatic relations between Ukraine and these countries.

This unprecedented focus on African countries and consistent political engagement demonstrate modern
Ukraine’s aspiration to effectively participate in the continent’s strategic processes. Ukraine aims to strengthen
ties with African nations, expand its presence, and foster cultural and people-to-people exchange. Given the
current geopolitical and economic landscape, Ukraine should build its relationships with African partners on
a systematic, flexible, and adaptable foundation. This necessitates a deep understanding of the complex and
diverse developmental realities of individual African states and pan-African integration organizations, as well
as the multifaceted nature of each sub-region.

Despite the geographical distance and cultural diversity of Africa, Ukraine and the countries of the continent
share a history of struggle for freedom, building independence, finding their place on the world stage, and
establishing state and national identity. However, the current geopolitical situation, including Russia’s full-scale
armed aggression against Ukraine and global security challenges for many African countries, has highlighted
the critical need to introduce new approaches to Ukraine’s communication with them. These approaches
should be grounded in a clear understanding of Africa’s pivotal role in the present and future global order, its
significance in Ukrainian foreign policy, and the imperative of enhancing Ukraine’s visibility on the continent.
Additionally, a comprehensive strategy to counter disinformation and propaganda is essential.

The first-ever Ukraine–African countries Communication Strategy (hereinafter referred to as the Strategy)
of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Ukraine aims to fully reflect the importance and diversity of Ukraine’s
relations with the countries of the continent and to establish mechanisms for a robust strategic partnership.
Several African countries are emerging as significant global players, and Ukraine should capitalize on this
potential by incorporating it into its economic, political, and diplomatic relations. It is essential to acknowledge
that Ukraine’s diplomacy has historically underemphasized comprehensive cooperation with African countries,
particularly those in West, Central, Eastern, and Southern Africa. A key objective for the next three years is to
rectify this oversight by transitioning from ad hoc cooperation to consistent and comprehensive diplomatic
engagement in all African regions.

The Strategy outlines specific steps aimed at deepening and expanding Ukraine’s communication with African
countries, including communication support for various initiatives to fight for the rule of law, strengthen the
economic independence of our societies, enhance food security, and increase trade, investment, and cultural
exchange.

Some unprecedented shifts in relations between Ukraine and African countries are already evident including
the following support for Ukraine by some previously emphatically neutral African countries during the UNGA
votes; increased diplomatic activity by several African leaders; proposals to resume grain exports; revitalization
of trade and economic activity, etc. However, Ukraine must intensify efforts to build upon these achievements.
Ukrainian policy in this area should be systematic, consistent, long-term, and effective.

As Ukraine seeks to forge stronger ties and meaningful cooperation with the dynamic African continent, it is
crucial to dispel outdated perceptions and acknowledge Africa’s multifaceted and diverse nature. We view
Africa not as a monolithic entity, but as a mosaic of 54 sovereign states, each possessing a unique blend of
political, economic, cultural, and religious identities. Recognizing the immense potential of African countries,
Ukraine is committed to establishing friendly and equitable relationships without intermediaries, revitalizing
existing bilateral and multilateral partnerships, and embarking on a new era of cooperation. Together, we
will strive for progress grounded in mutual respect, shared interests, and reciprocal benefits. Strategic
communications are indispensable for laying the groundwork and facilitating the implementation of our new
policy towards Africa. While we are only beginning this journey, we are confident it will lead to a flourishing
partnership and a shared future characterized by inclusivity, justice, and prosperity.

Framework for Developing the Strategy

In development of the Strategy, we relied on the Communication Strategy of the MFA of Ukraine (March
2021), the Public Diplomacy Strategy of the MFA of Ukraine (March 2021), the Strategy of Ukraine’s Relations
with African States (January 2022), and several analytical studies conducted by the MFA and expert non-
governmental organizations. The Department of Public Diplomacy and Communication of the Ministry of
Foreign Affairs of Ukraine conducted a general analysis of media consumption in Africa, an analysis of local
media languages, an audit of the communication capabilities of Ukraine’s diplomatic missions in Africa, and
the current staffing situation. Additionally, the MFA’s monitoring and analytical data on Ukraine-related
narratives in African media during 2022-2023 served as valuable input.

The Term of Action and Its Justification

                       The Strategy is valid for 3 years

Despite Russia’s unprovoked full-scale aggression against Ukraine and ongoing efforts to liberate Ukrainian
territories, Ukraine remains steadfast in pursuing its strategic interests in Africa and developing a mutually
beneficial post-war partnership. This Strategy outlines a roadmap for 2024-2026.
A three-year timeframe offers sufficient opportunity to implement and assess the effectiveness of various
communication initiatives, enabling adjustments in response to evolving conditions, such as political and
economic developments in Ukraine, African countries, and globally. By setting clear communication objectives
and timelines for engagement with African nations, Ukraine can optimize its position to achieve long-term
sustainable development and mutually beneficial partnerships on the continent.
After the end of the war, shaping Ukraine’s post-war image in Africa will be a paramount focus for Ukraine
and the MFA. Strategically, efforts will center on positioning Ukraine as a dependable and influential political
and economic partner, countering disinformation and propaganda, and establishing or reforming international
platforms for multilateral cooperation grounded in justice, peace, friendship, equality, and transparency. A
top priority will be the multifaceted development of economic ties, including leveraging Ukraine’s agricultural
strengths to address Africa’s food security challenges.

Political and Economic
Zoning of Africa

We believe it is appropriate to align Ukraine’s regional framework for Africa with that of the African Union.
When dividing into regions, the MFA relies on the unchanged official version of the regionalization of Africa,
which was defined by the Organization of African Unity (OAU) in 1976. According to it, the member states of
the African Union are divided into five geographical regions:

  North Africa
  West Africa
  Central Africa
  East Africa
  South Africa

Prioritization of communications in each African sub-region was based on the
probability of achieving desired outcomes within the Strategy’s timeframe.

A 2023 internal audit of communication capacity at diplomatic missions revealed that each African mission
currently designates a media relations officer. However, due to the limited staffing situation, all these officers
are also responsible for other areas. While most diplomats are proficient in one or two official languages
of their host country, this is frequently insufficient given the number of countries under their purview and
the prevalence of local languages. For instance, the Embassy in South Africa covers ten countries where, in
addition to English, French, or Portuguese, dozens of other languages hold official status.

Communication challenges also exist in languages widely spoken by citizens in accreditation and host countries,
including Hausa, Igbo, and Yoruba (Nigeria); Zulu, Xhosa, and Sotho (South Africa); Wolof (Senegal); Swahili
(Kenya, Tanzania); Amazigh/Tamazight (Morocco); Amharic and Oromo (Ethiopia); Kinyarwanda (Rwanda);
Akan (Ghana, Côte d’Ivoire); Lingala (DR Congo); and others. To address this, Ukraine’s foreign diplomatic
missions should bolster their capacity by hiring language specialists and partnering with local communication
experts.
