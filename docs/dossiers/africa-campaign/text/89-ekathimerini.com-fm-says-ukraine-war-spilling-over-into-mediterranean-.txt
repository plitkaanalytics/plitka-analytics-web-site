# FM says Ukraine war spilling over into Mediterranean ‘will not be tolerated’

Greece on Wednesday escalated diplomatic pressure on Kyiv after a suspected Ukrainian naval drone was discovered off the island of Lefkada last week, with Foreign Minister George Gerapetritis saying that “any transformation of the Mediterranean into a theater of military operations will not be tolerated.”

Following talks in Athens with his Croatian counterpart Gordan Grlic Radman, Gerapetritis added that “Greece will ensure that the Mediterranean remains untouched by military operations and remains a sea of peace, a sea belonging to its peoples.”

The remarks reflected Athens’ growing concern over the incident involving a naval drone found near the Ionian island of Lefkada on Thursday, an episode Greek officials view as evidence of the growing risks posed by the war in Ukraine extending beyond the Black Sea.

Gerapetritis also said the European Union faced unprecedented security challenges after four years of active conflicts across the wider region.

“The greatest challenge, of course, is preventing the spread of the wars that are troubling our neighborhood,” he said.

Greek officials have intensified calls for explanations from Kyiv in recent days, with Defense Minister Nikos Dendias and government spokesman Pavlos Marinakis also publicly raising concerns.

Athens maintains the sea drone was deployed in Greece’s territorial waters by Ukraine in an apparent attempt to strike a Russian vessel in the Mediterranean. Ukrainian authorities have twice denied having information about the vessel, which was packed with explosives.

During a videoconference Tuesday between defense ministers from the EU’s 27 member states and Ukrainian officials, Ukrainian Defense Minister Mykhailo Fedorov declined to comment when Dendias raised the issue, Greek officials said. Earlier, Ukrainian Foreign Ministry spokesman Heorhii Tykhyi said Kyiv had no information regarding the drone discovered in Greece.

Investigations into the device are continuing at facilities in Skaramagas near Athens, where military authorities are examining the drone’s systems and mission data. Greek officials have preliminarily identified the vessel as a Mamai-type kamikaze naval drone, a model widely used by Ukrainian forces in the Black Sea during the war.

Initial work focused on removing explosives and detonators from the drone, which is now being held at the facilities of Greece’s Underwater Demolition Command. The investigation is being led by the Hellenic National Defense General Staff, although coast guard personnel carried out some initial inspections.

Greek authorities are seeking rapid conclusions from the inquiry as diplomatic contacts with Kyiv continue. Athens has also briefed close allies in both NATO and the EU about what it sees as the dangers of any expansion of the Black Sea conflict into the Mediterranean or Aegean seas.

Greek officials are particularly concerned about the economic and environmental consequences of any future attack near the country’s coastline, including the possibility of strikes on commercial tankers operating in nearby waters.
