The Head of the Libyan Institution for Human Rights, Ahmed Hamza, revealed information he said had been confirmed by reliable sources indicating a direct link between a cell whose members were arrested in connection with drone attacks targeting vital facilities in western Libya and Omar Marajea, a commander in Khalifa Haftar’s forces.

Hamza said in a Facebook post that the cell included a foreign mercenary military expert who had previously carried out similar operations in South Sudan and the city of El Fasher. He said the cell was involved in attacks targeting oil facilities, power stations and water networks in Al-Zawiya, as well as an attempted attack on the Brega oil depot on Airport Road in Tripoli.

He added that the cell was directly linked to Omar Marajea, whom he described as a commander in Khalifa Haftar’s forces. Hamza accused Haftar-affiliated entities of bringing foreign experts into Libya through Benina International Airport and providing funding for military sabotage operations in western Libya.

According to Hamza, citing his sources, the objective of these activities was not limited to targeting vital infrastructure. He said the incidents were also intended to be exploited politically and through the media to convey “distorted messages” about the political and security situation in western Libya, while creating crises involving oil, electricity and water and exploiting their repercussions for the benefit of those involved.

The Head of the Libyan Institution for Human Rights called for expanding the ongoing investigations into the cell’s members to identify those who cooperated with them and facilitated their entry into Libya, as well as those who supplied the drones used in the attacks, financed the operations and issued the orders.

He also called for an investigation into the parties from whom the cell received its instructions, citing information about attempts to pay up to 50 million Libyan dinars to kill the cell members, eliminate them and bury their secrets in order to conceal the nature of their connections.

Hamza further cited information about an attempt to smuggle the cell members out of Al-Zawiya in exchange for 10 million Libyan dinars. He called for these allegations to be examined as part of the official investigations and for all those involved in the case, as well as the parties behind it, to be identified.
