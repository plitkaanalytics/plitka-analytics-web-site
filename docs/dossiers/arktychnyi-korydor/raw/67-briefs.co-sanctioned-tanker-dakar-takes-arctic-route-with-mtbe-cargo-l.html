Sanctioned tanker Dakar takes Arctic route with MTBE cargo likely headed for Russia

Jackson Moreland
Published Oct 7, 2026
Share:
Summary:
  • Following a ship-to-ship handoff near South Korea in early September, the EU-blacklisted Dakar is headed west along Russia's Arctic shoreline with a cargo of MTBE.
  • Kpler figures indicate a summer surge in Asian fuel shipments to Russia: over 1.2 million barrels in July, then roughly 700,000 from South Korea and about 320,000 from India in August.
  • The rush reflects refinery outages in Russia after intensified Ukrainian strikes, and as Asia nears the winter season the pace is easing; preliminary reads indicate India shipped zero barrels to Russia in September.

What's on the move and why it matters

A small tanker named Dakar, which the European Union placed under sanctions earlier this year, is traveling west along Russia's Arctic shoreline with a load of methyl tert-butyl ether, or MTBE. Kpler's tracking shows the additive was first loaded in China onto the Yorik in early August, then shifted via ship-to-ship transfer to the Dakar near Yeosu, South Korea, in the first part of September.

The vessel isn't broadcasting a destination. Given its sanctioned status, major European ports are largely off limits, making Russia the plausible end point. Bloomberg also observed the Dakar leaving South Korea in the first days of September and proceeding along the Arctic coast.

The bigger picture: Asia's fuel lifeline to Russia

Russia is normally a net exporter of fuels like diesel. But stepped-up Ukrainian strikes have crimped refining and led to shortages at home, drawing in supplies from Asia. Kpler's numbers show a notable jump in July and August: more than 1.2 million barrels arrived in July, mostly sourced from India, followed in August by around 700,000 barrels from South Korea and about 320,000 from India. Before that burst, monthly shipments hadn't cleared 150,000 barrels since the 2022 invasion.

One ship in particular, the Aram Khachaturian, stood out with three runs across July and August totaling roughly 260,000 barrels of refined product, making it the busiest tanker over that stretch. Nine other carriers were involved too, some under EU and UK sanctions, with several deliveries executed via ship-to-ship transfers.

Sanctioned cargoes find new routes, and those routes carry their own costs. Market Briefs covers energy trade free every morning.

Arctic passage, logistics, and ownership details

The Northern Sea Route is usually sailed by ships built for ice conditions, but in warmer months even sanctioned vessels that lack an ice-class rating, such as the Dakar, have completed the journey. The observed summer deliveries from South Korea and India did not use the Arctic corridor.

Who exactly sent the recent cargoes from Asia remains unclear. The Guardian first brought the broader shipment pattern to light.

Records in Equasis list Vulcano Marine SA as the Dakar's owner and show it shares an address with its manager, Ultramarine Navigation Management, in Dubai. Someone who answered a call placed to Vulcano Marine said the number was incorrect, and Ultramarine Navigation had no other contact details listed.

What this could mean for your wallet

Following the summer surge, flows have cooled as Asia approaches the Northern Hemisphere winter. Initial Kpler readings indicate India recorded zero exports to Russia in September, though voyages can take more than a month, so additional arrivals may still register. The fuels tracked in these flows span refined petroleum products and fuel oil.

Big picture, when Russia needs to import gasoline components like MTBE and more refined fuel shows up from Asia, it reshuffles routes, freight rates, and margins. For everyday investors, watching volumes, origin points like Ulsan, and repeat-visitor tankers is a simple way to gauge where the shipping and fuel trade is tightening or loosening.
