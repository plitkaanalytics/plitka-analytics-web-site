Commercial use of the Northern Sea Route accelerated materially during the 2026 navigation season, but the corridor remains far from becoming a year-round substitute for Suez. The Centre for High North Logistics had identified 104 confirmed NSR transit voyages by September 30, already exceeding the 103 recorded during all of 2025, with approximately 3.7 million tonnes of transit cargo and another 45 vessels either inside the route or on voyages expected to transit it. CHNL says the season could approach 150 transits and more than 4.5 million tonnes of cargo if most of those voyages are completed. Container activity is also becoming more structured: China ran its first season of recurring weekly Asia-Europe Arctic sailings, while South Korea has just completed its first commercial container round trip between Busan and Europe. Yet direct Asia-Europe traffic remains a small minority of total NSR activity, the navigable season is still highly seasonal, and cargo density, insurance, Russian coordination, ice variability and sanctions exposure continue to complicate the economics of regular liner service.

## Arctic Shipping Is Scaling, But It Is Scaling Unevenly

Tankers and bulk carriers remain the backbone of Northern Sea Route traffic, while container shipping is moving from isolated demonstrations toward scheduled seasonal operations.

**Important:**the approximately 150-transit figure is an interim scenario, not a final 2026 result. CHNL's confirmed count was 104 as of September 30 and the navigation season was still active.

## What Changed During the 2026 Season

The Arctic story is no longer only about shrinking distance. China, South Korea and Russia are now building different commercial, financial and logistics structures around the route.
