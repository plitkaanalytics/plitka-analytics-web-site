# Bellingcat Style Guide — Reference

## Headline Formulas

Bellingcat headlines follow a few consistent patterns. Study these and adapt:

**Attribution pattern:**
> "Russian Soldier Photographed at Ukrainian Border Identified as Member of 64th Motor Rifle Brigade"

**Finding pattern:**
> "Satellite Imagery Shows New Russian Military Buildup Near Kursk"

**Exposure pattern:**
> "How We Identified the GRU Officers Behind the Salisbury Poisoning"

**Debunk pattern:**
> "No, This Video Does Not Show Ukrainian Forces Shelling Civilians"

**Ukrainian equivalents:**
> «Встановлено особу російського військового, сфотографованого в Херсоні»
> «Супутникові знімки показують нові укріплення поблизу Токмака»
> «Як ми ідентифікували офіцерів ФСБ, причетних до операції в Маріуполі»

---

## The Bellingcat Lead Formula

The lead (перший абзац) must do three things:
1. State the **most important confirmed finding**
2. Establish **why it matters**
3. Hint at **how it was discovered**

Example:
> "Bellingcat and its investigative partners have identified a Russian military officer whose unit is responsible for the shelling of a residential neighborhood in Zaporizhzhia on 15 March, using a combination of open-source intelligence and financial records obtained from leaked databases."

Ukrainian:
> «Редакція встановила особу офіцера російської армії, підрозділ якого несе відповідальність за обстріл житлового кварталу в Запоріжжі 15 березня. Ідентифікацію проведено на основі відкритих даних та фінансових документів з витоклих баз даних.»

## Evidence Blocks — Formatting

When presenting a piece of evidence, use this three-part structure in the text:

1. **Introduce the evidence**: what it is, where it came from, when
2. **Describe what it shows**: specific, factual, observed — not interpreted
3. **State its significance**: what this tells us about the central allegation

Example:
> *[INTRODUCE]* На відео, опублікованому 23 лютого у Telegram-каналі «Воєнний кореспондент» (архів), зафіксовано колону бронетехніки.
> *[DESCRIBE]* На кадрах видно щонайменше три танки типу Т-72 та два бронетранспортери БТР-82А з номерними знаками серії «02».
> *[SIGNIFICANCE]* Серія «02» відповідає реєстрації транспортних засобів Республіки Інгушетія, що вказує на можливу приналежність до підрозділів Південного військового округу.

## Hedging and Certainty Language — Extended List

### Ukrainian
- **Встановлено** — confirmed (use sparingly, only when truly certain)
- **Підтверджено** — verified
- **Свідчать дані** — the data indicates
- **Є підстави вважати** — there are grounds to believe
- **Схоже на те, що** — it appears that
- **Можливо** — possibly (weak)
- **Імовірно** — probably (moderate)
- **Не виключено** — cannot be ruled out
- **Редакція не змогла підтвердити** — the editorial team could not confirm
- **За наявними даними** — based on available information

### English
- **Confirmed** — only with multiple independent sources
- **Verified** — checked against at least one corroborating source
- **Evidence suggests / indicates** — strong inference
- **Appears to / Seems to** — probable but not confirmed
- **May / Might** — possible
- **Could not be independently verified** — standard disclaimer
- **According to [source]** — attributed, not independently verified by outlet
- **Claimed by / Alleged** — treat with skepticism, awaiting verification

## Archive and Citation Standards

**Always archive before citing.** Web content disappears. Standard tools:
- `web.archive.org` — Wayback Machine
- `archive.ph` — fast, reliable for social media
- `cachedview.nl` — Google cache

**Citation format in text (Ukrainian):**
> (Telegram-канал N, 15.03.2024; архів: archive.ph/XXXXX)

**Citation format in text (English):**
> (Telegram channel N, March 15, 2024; archived at archive.ph/XXXXX)

**For documents:** note the document number, issuing authority, date, and how obtained.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Headline-evidence mismatch** — the headline claims more than the article proves. Always walk back the headline to the strongest *confirmed* finding.

2. **Attribution creep** — starting with "allegedly" then forgetting to re-hedge later in the text.

3. **Passive identification** — "The individual is believed to be X" with no explanation of the basis. Always state *why* you believe this.

4. **Screenshot without context** — showing a screenshot without explaining: what platform, what account, what date, whether the account is verified/notable.

5. **Geolocation without methodology** — stating a location without explaining how it was determined.

6. **Missing the alternative explanation** — good investigations consider and address the strongest counter-argument or alternative interpretation of the evidence.

7. **Over-hedging** — so many qualifiers that the reader can't understand what you're actually claiming. Find the right level of certainty and commit to it.

## Structure Cheat Sheet

```
HEADLINE: [Subject] [Verb] [Object/Finding]
DECK: One sentence expanding on the headline.

LEAD (2-3 sentences):
  - Finding
  - Why it matters
  - Method hint

CONTEXT (2-4 paragraphs):
  - Background
  - Previous related events
  - Who are the actors

METHODOLOGY (1-2 paragraphs):
  - What sources were used
  - How evidence was verified
  - Any notable limitations

FINDINGS (main body, as many sections as needed):
  Per section:
    - Subheading (optional)
    - Claim
    - Evidence block(s)
    - Significance

RESPONSE (if applicable):
  - Subject's statement or non-response

CONCLUSION:
  - What this proves
  - What remains unknown
  - What should happen / next steps

NOTES / SOURCES:
  - Full source list with archive links
```
